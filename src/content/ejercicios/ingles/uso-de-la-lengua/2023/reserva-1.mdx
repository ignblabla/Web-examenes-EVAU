<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“Remember… your passport with you to confirm your identity.”</strong> (bring)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“Look at those monkeys! I… a picture before they run away!”</strong> (take)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “Remember <strong>to bring</strong> your passport with you to confirm your identity.”</p>
    <p><em>Remember to</em> + infinitivo significa “acordarse de hacer algo” que todavía no se ha hecho.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “Look at those monkeys! I <strong>will take</strong> (o <strong>’ll take</strong>) a picture before they run away!”</p>
    <p>Se usa <em>will</em> para una decisión espontánea tomada en el momento de hablar.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“Since the lift was out of order I… use the stairs.”</strong> ought to / had to / could / must</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“Please, take your umbrella… it rains.”</strong> in case / as much as / as long as / in case of</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “Since the lift was out of order I <strong>had to</strong> use the stairs.”</p>
    <p>Es una obligación en el pasado, así que se usa <em>had to</em>; <em>must</em> no tiene forma de pasado.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “Please, take your umbrella <strong>in case</strong> it rains.”</p>
    <p><em>In case</em> (“por si”) va seguido de una oración; <em>in case of</em> va seguido de un nombre.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THIS SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“The Cabinet has already been reorganised by the Prime Minister.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The Prime Minister has already reorganised the Cabinet.</strong></p>
    <p>El agente (<em>by the Prime Minister</em>) pasa a ser el sujeto y se mantiene el present perfect con <em>already</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THIS SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“Scientists have to conduct a new study to evaluate the situation after the volcano’s eruption.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>A new study has to be conducted to evaluate the situation after the volcano’s eruption.</strong> (o <strong>A new study to evaluate the situation after the volcano’s eruption has to be conducted.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Con <em>have to</em>, la pasiva se forma con <em>has to be</em> + participio. El objeto pasa a ser el sujeto y el agente <em>scientists</em> se puede omitir.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“She’s been in charge <u>for a couple of years</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>(For) How long has she been in charge?</strong></p>
    <p>Para preguntar por una duración se usa <em>how long</em>, con el auxiliar <em>has</em> delante del sujeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“The knife had a really sharp blade. I got cut with it.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The knife with which I got cut had a really sharp blade.</strong> (o <strong>I got cut with the knife(,) which had a really sharp blade.</strong>)</p>
    <p>El pronombre <em>it</em> se sustituye por <em>which</em>; si la preposición <em>with</em> va delante, el relativo tiene que ser <em>which</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE NEXT SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“This coat is too heavy to be worn all day long.”</strong> This coat isn’t...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>This coat isn’t light enough to be worn all day long.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Too</em> + adjetivo equivale a <em>not</em> + adjetivo contrario + <em>enough</em>: <em>too heavy</em> → <em>not light enough</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THIS CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“… , provided that you help me.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I will finish the project today, provided that you help me.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p><em>Provided that</em> (“siempre que”) introduce una condición en presente, como un primer condicional: la oración principal lleva sujeto + <em>will</em> + infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“Despite my complaint, I did not get a pay rise.”</strong> Although...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Although I complained (o had complained), I did not get a pay rise.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Despite</em> va seguido de un nombre (<em>my complaint</em>); <em>although</em> necesita una oración con sujeto y verbo: <em>I complained</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“In addition with making his bed, my little brother always helps me with many others household chores.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>In addition <u>to</u> making his bed, my little brother always helps me with many <u>other</u> household chores.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: la expresión es <em>in addition to</em> (+ -ing). Segundo error: <em>other</em> delante de un nombre es adjetivo y no lleva <em>-s</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO DIRECT SPEECH: <strong>“The coach asked us why we had lost focus during the previous day’s match.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>“Why did you lose focus during yesterday’s match?” the coach asked (us).</strong> (o <strong>The coach said (to us) / told us / asked (us), “Why did you lose focus during yesterday’s match?”</strong>)</p>
    <p>En estilo directo la pregunta recupera el orden interrogativo con <em>did</em>, el pasado perfecto vuelve a pasado simple, <em>we</em> pasa a <em>you</em> y <em>the previous day’s</em> a <em>yesterday’s</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>will</span><span>my</span><span>by</span><span>done</span><span>I</span><span>get</span><span>professional</span><span>a</span><span>nails</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I will get my nails done by a professional.</strong></p>
    <p>Estructura causativa <em>get</em> + objeto + participio, en futuro, con el agente introducido por <em>by</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
