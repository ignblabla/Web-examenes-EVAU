<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“You should… him more nicely when he visited you. Now he’s upset.”</strong> (treat)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“By the time we get married next summer, we… for two years.”</strong> (be engaged)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “You should <strong>have treated</strong> him more nicely when he visited you. Now he’s upset.”</p>
    <p>Para criticar o lamentar algo que no se hizo en el pasado se usa <em>should have</em> + participio.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “By the time we get married next summer, we <strong>will have been engaged</strong> for two years.”</p>
    <p>Con <em>by the time</em> + momento futuro y <em>for</em> + periodo se usa el futuro perfecto (<em>will have</em> + participio): el compromiso habrá durado dos años cuando llegue la boda.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“Can I… your charger? My phone battery has died.”</strong> lend / own / borrow / let</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“He escaped by digging a hole… the wall.”</strong> through / out / thorough / around</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “Can I <strong>borrow</strong> your charger? My phone battery has died.”</p>
    <p><em>Borrow</em> es “tomar prestado”: quien habla es quien recibe el cargador. <em>Lend</em> (“prestar”) se usaría con <em>you</em> como sujeto: <em>Can you lend me…?</em></p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “He escaped by digging a hole <strong>through</strong> the wall.”</p>
    <p><em>Through</em> indica que el agujero atraviesa la pared de un lado a otro. No hay que confundirlo con <em>thorough</em> (“minucioso”).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THIS SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“The rumour was being spread by all the media outlets.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>All the media outlets were spreading the rumour.</strong></p>
    <p>El agente (<em>by all the media outlets</em>) pasa a ser el sujeto y <em>the rumour</em> pasa a ser el objeto. Se mantiene el pasado continuo: <em>was being spread</em> → <em>were spreading</em>, en plural porque el nuevo sujeto es plural.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THIS SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“We ask students to mute their phones while in class.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Students are asked to mute their phones while in class.</strong></p>
    <p>El objeto (<em>students</em>) pasa a ser el sujeto, con el presente simple pasivo <em>are asked</em>. El complemento <em>to mute their phones</em> se mantiene y el agente <em>we</em> se omite.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“Cecilia is <u>pretty, short and has big blue eyes</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>What is Cecilia like?</strong></p>
    <p>Para pedir una descripción de cómo es una persona se usa <em>What is… like?</em>. No hay que confundirlo con <em>What does Cecilia like?</em>, que pregunta por sus gustos.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“This is the hospital. Your sister was born here.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>This is the hospital where (o in which) your sister was born.</strong> (o <strong>This is the hospital (which/that) your sister was born in.</strong>)</p>
    <p>El adverbio de lugar <em>here</em> se sustituye por <em>where</em> o <em>in which</em>. También se puede usar <em>which/that</em>, o ningún relativo, dejando la preposición <em>in</em> al final.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE NEXT SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“It wasn’t necessary for you to work late last night.”</strong> You needn’t…</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You needn’t have worked late last night.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Needn’t have</em> + participio indica que alguien hizo algo en el pasado que no era necesario: trabajaste hasta tarde, pero no hacía falta.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THIS CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“If I had known you were busy, …”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If I had known you were busy, I wouldn’t have called you.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Es un tercer condicional, sobre una situación pasada que no ocurrió: con <em>If</em> + pasado perfecto (<em>had known</em>), la oración principal lleva sujeto + <em>would (not) have</em> + participio. Cualquier final con sentido y con esa estructura es correcto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“When did they buy their new sports car?”</strong> How long...?</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How long have they had their new sports car?</strong></p>
    <p>Con <em>how long</em> se pregunta por una duración que llega hasta el presente, así que se usa el present perfect. <em>Buy</em> es una acción puntual, por eso se sustituye por <em>have</em> (“tener”), que expresa un estado que dura: <em>have had</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“Can you please put all this books in the shelf above?”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Can you please put all <u>these</u> books <u>on</u> the shelf above?</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: <em>books</em> es plural, así que el demostrativo es <em>these</em>, no <em>this</em>. Segundo error: con una superficie como una balda se usa <em>on</em>, no <em>in</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO DIRECT SPEECH: <strong>“The manager told me to discuss my idea with the senior clerk.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>“Discuss your idea with the senior clerk (, please),” the manager asked / told me.</strong></p>
    <p>Una orden en estilo indirecto (<em>told me to</em> + infinitivo) se convierte en un imperativo entre comillas. El posesivo cambia de <em>my</em> a <em>your</em>, porque ahora es el jefe quien habla.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>back</span><span>it</span><span>to</span><span>time</span><span>home</span><span>think</span><span>is</span><span>I</span><span>go</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I think it is time to go back home.</strong></p>
    <p>La estructura <em>it is time to</em> + infinitivo significa “es hora de”, y va introducida por <em>I think</em>. <em>Go back home</em> (“volver a casa”) va sin preposición delante de <em>home</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
