<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“I had the gas pipes… last week.”</strong> (check)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“I’m looking forward to… promoted at work.”</strong> (be)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “I had the gas pipes <strong>checked</strong> last week.”</p>
    <p>Estructura causativa <em>have</em> + objeto + participio: no las revisé yo, sino que encargué a alguien que lo hiciera.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “I’m looking forward to <strong>being</strong> promoted at work.”</p>
    <p>En <em>look forward to</em>, <em>to</em> es una preposición, así que va seguido de -ing: <em>being promoted</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“My car broke… and that’s why I was late.”</strong> off / down / on / up</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“Your sister is still mad… me.”</strong> at / to / after / with</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “My car broke <strong>down</strong> and that’s why I was late.”</p>
    <p>El <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>break down</em> significa “averiarse, dejar de funcionar”, sobre todo un vehículo o una máquina.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “Your sister is still mad <strong>at</strong> me.”</p>
    <p><em>Be mad at somebody</em> significa “estar enfadado con alguien”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THIS SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“The cows are being milked now.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Someone is milking the cows now.</strong> (o <strong>They are milking the cows now.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Como la pasiva no indica quién hace la acción, en activa se usa un sujeto genérico (<em>someone</em> o <em>they</em>). Se mantiene el presente continuo: <em>are being milked</em> → <em>is/are milking</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THIS SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“Unfortunately, they don’t believe Jack is a great musician.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Unfortunately, Jack isn’t believed to be a great musician.</strong> (o <strong>Unfortunately, it isn’t believed that Jack is a great musician.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Con verbos de opinión como <em>believe</em> hay dos pasivas: con sujeto personal (<em>Jack isn’t believed</em> + <em>to</em>-infinitivo) o impersonal (<em>it isn’t believed that</em> + oración). El sujeto genérico <em>they</em> desaparece.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“The president is giving an interview <u>at a local TV channel</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Where is the president giving an interview?</strong></p>
    <p>Las palabras subrayadas indican un lugar, así que se pregunta con <em>where</em>. En presente continuo, el auxiliar <em>is</em> va delante del sujeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“Paris is known as the City of Light. I’d like to go there.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Paris, where I’d like to go, is known as the City of Light.</strong> (o <strong>I’d like to go to Paris, which is known as the City of Light.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Como <em>Paris</em> es un nombre propio, la relativa es explicativa y va entre comas. Si el relativo sustituye a <em>there</em>, se usa <em>where</em>; si sustituye a <em>Paris</em> como sujeto, <em>which</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE NEXT SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“I’m sure Jim was in the garden when the phone rang.”</strong> Jim must...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Jim must have been in the garden when the phone rang.</strong></p>
    <p>Para expresar una deducción casi segura sobre el pasado se usa <em>must have</em> + participio. Sustituye a <em>I’m sure</em> + pasado simple.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THIS CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“What would you tell her...?”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>What would you tell her if you saw her again?</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Es un segundo condicional, para una situación hipotética: si la oración principal lleva <em>would</em> + infinitivo, la condición va con <em>if</em> + sujeto + pasado simple. Cualquier condición con sentido y con esa estructura es correcta.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“Nobody won a medal except Jane.”</strong> The only…</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The only person/one who/that won a medal was Jane.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Nobody… except Jane</em> equivale a “la única persona que…”: <em>the only person/one</em> + relativa especificativa con <em>who</em> o <em>that</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“I asked to them where had they parked the day before.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I asked them where they had parked the day before.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: <em>ask</em> va seguido directamente de la persona, sin <em>to</em>. Segundo error: en una pregunta indirecta no hay inversión; el sujeto va delante del verbo: <em>where they had parked</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘Shall we go to the cinema?’ Jane asked.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Jane suggested going to the cinema.</strong> (o <strong>Jane suggested that we (could/should) go to the cinema.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Shall we…?</em> es una sugerencia, así que en estilo indirecto se usa el verbo <em>suggest</em>, seguido de -ing o de <em>that</em> + oración.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>is</span><span>only</span><span>to</span><span>deal</span><span>talking</span><span>with</span><span>the</span><span>way</span><span>problems</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Talking is the only way to deal with problems.</strong></p>
    <p>El sujeto es el gerundio <em>talking</em>. Detrás va <em>the only way to</em> + infinitivo (“la única forma de”) y el verbo con preposición <em>deal with</em> (“afrontar”).</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
