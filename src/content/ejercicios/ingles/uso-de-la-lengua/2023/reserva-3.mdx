<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“Susan gave up… five years ago.”</strong> (jog)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“No, the word <em>stunning</em>… that!”</strong> (not mean)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “Susan gave up <strong>jogging</strong> five years ago.”</p>
    <p>El <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>give up</em> (“dejar de”) va seguido de -ing.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “No, the word <em>stunning</em> <strong>does not</strong> (o <strong>doesn’t</strong>) <strong>mean</strong> that!”</p>
    <p>Es una verdad general, así que se usa el presente simple negativo, con <em>does</em> porque el sujeto (<em>the word</em>) es singular.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“… people sing country music in Spain.”</strong> A lot / Few / Much / Little</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“My brother is coming to pick me… at the bus station.”</strong> after / up / in / on</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “<strong>Few</strong> people sing country music in Spain.”</p>
    <p><em>People</em> es contable, así que se descartan <em>much</em> y <em>little</em>; <em>a lot</em> necesitaría <em>of</em>. <em>Few</em> significa “pocos”.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “My brother is coming to pick me <strong>up</strong> at the bus station.”</p>
    <p>El <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>pick up</em> significa “recoger a alguien”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THIS SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“When her parents passed away, she was brought up by a foster family.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>When her parents passed away, a foster family brought her up.</strong></p>
    <p>El agente (<em>by a foster family</em>) pasa a ser el sujeto y <em>she</em> pasa a <em>her</em>, como objeto del <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>bring up</em> (“criar”).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THIS SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“Did they make these high-end clothes in Spain in the 1990s?”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Were these high-end clothes made in Spain in the 1990s?</strong></p>
    <p>Pasiva interrogativa en pasado simple: <em>were</em> + sujeto + participio. Se usa <em>were</em> porque <em>clothes</em> es plural.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“They used to go to the cinema <u>every two weeks</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How often did they use to go to the cinema?</strong></p>
    <p>Para preguntar por la frecuencia se usa <em>how often</em>. En interrogativa, <em>used to</em> se forma con <em>did</em> + <em>use to</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“Mary has got a new boyfriend. His mother is a very well-known writer.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Mary has got a new boyfriend whose mother is a very well-known writer.</strong></p>
    <p>El posesivo <em>his</em> se sustituye por el relativo posesivo <em>whose</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE NEXT SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“We suggest that we make a move today.”</strong> What about...?</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>What about making a move today?</strong></p>
    <p>Una sugerencia se puede expresar con <em>What about</em> + -ing, en forma de pregunta.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THIS CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“If she dares to do that again, …”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If she dares to do that again, she will be punished.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Primer condicional: con <em>if</em> + presente simple, la oración principal lleva sujeto + <em>will</em> + infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“First, Sophie warmed up for ten minutes. Then, she worked out for two hours.”</strong> After...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>After warming up for ten minutes, Sophie worked out for two hours.</strong> (o <strong>After she/Sophie (had) warmed up for ten minutes, she/Sophie worked out for two hours.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>After</em> puede ir seguido de -ing (si el sujeto es el mismo) o de una oración; el pasado perfecto subraya que el calentamiento fue anterior.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“A friend of us has worked like a hairdresser in the past, but he wouldn’t like to do it again.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>A friend of <u>ours</u> has worked <u>as</u> a hairdresser in the past, but he wouldn’t like to do it again.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: <em>a friend of</em> va seguido de un pronombre posesivo (<em>ours</em>). Segundo error: para una profesión se usa <em>as</em> (“como, en calidad de”), no <em>like</em> (“parecido a”).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘We won’t meet our new literature teacher tomorrow,’ the students said.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The students said that they wouldn’t meet their new literature teacher the following day / the next day / the day after.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Won’t</em> retrocede a <em>wouldn’t</em>, <em>we/our</em> pasan a <em>they/their</em> y <em>tomorrow</em> a <em>the following day</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>did</span><span>long</span><span>for</span><span>you</span><span>come</span><span>wait</span><span>the</span><span>train</span><span>to</span><span>how</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How long did you wait for the train to come?</strong></p>
    <p>Pregunta por la duración con <em>how long</em> + <em>did</em>. La estructura <em>wait for</em> + algo + <em>to</em>-infinitivo significa “esperar a que algo ocurra”.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
