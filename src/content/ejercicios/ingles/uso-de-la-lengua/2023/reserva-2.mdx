<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“She is so funny! She always makes me…”</strong> (laugh)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“I wish I… at him yesterday.”</strong> (not shout)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “She is so funny! She always makes me <strong>laugh</strong>.”</p>
    <p><em>Make</em> + objeto va seguido de infinitivo sin <em>to</em>.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “I wish I <strong>hadn’t shouted</strong> at him yesterday.”</p>
    <p>Para lamentar algo del pasado se usa <em>wish</em> + pasado perfecto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“The students will benefit… grants to study abroad.”</strong> at / from / of / for</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“He… an old friend at the shopping centre.”</strong> came up / came into / ran into / ran off</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “The students will benefit <strong>from</strong> grants to study abroad.”</p>
    <p><em>Benefit from</em> significa “beneficiarse de”.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “He <strong>ran into</strong> an old friend at the shopping centre.”</p>
    <p>El <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>run into</em> significa “encontrarse por casualidad con alguien”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THIS SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“Do you know that Martha was offered the position of chief officer?”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Do you know that they (o the firm) offered Martha the position of chief officer?</strong></p>
    <p>Solo cambia la oración subordinada. Como no se dice quién ofreció el puesto, se usa <em>they</em> o el agente lógico (<em>the firm</em>), y Martha pasa a ser el objeto indirecto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THIS SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“They must sweep the floor every day after work.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The floor must be swept every day after work.</strong></p>
    <p>Con un modal, la pasiva se forma con modal + <em>be</em> + participio (<em>swept</em>, participio irregular de <em>sweep</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORD: <strong>“<u>Peter’s</u> daughter won a song contest.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Whose daughter won a song contest?</strong></p>
    <p>Para preguntar por el poseedor se usa <em>whose</em> + nombre. Como se pregunta por el sujeto, no se usa el auxiliar <em>did</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“That is the flowerpot. I put the key under that pot.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>That is the flowerpot under which I put the key.</strong> (o <strong>That is the flowerpot (which) I put the key under.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>That pot</em> se sustituye por <em>which</em>. La preposición <em>under</em> puede ir delante del relativo o al final; en este último caso, el relativo se puede omitir.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE NEXT SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“The mechanic is fixing my car right now.”</strong> I am...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I am having (o getting) my car fixed right now (by a mechanic).</strong></p>
    <p>Estructura causativa <em>have/get</em> + objeto + participio en presente continuo: yo encargo la reparación y la hace otro.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THIS SENTENCE: <strong>“The harder you work, ...”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The harder you work, the more you will achieve.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>La estructura <em>the</em> + comparativo…, <em>the</em> + comparativo (“cuanto más…, más…”) exige que la segunda parte empiece también por <em>the</em> + comparativo, seguido de sujeto y verbo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“It’s impossible that you saw John yesterday because he was in London.”</strong> You...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You can’t have seen John yesterday because he was in London.</strong></p>
    <p>Para una deducción negativa segura sobre el pasado se usa <em>can’t have</em> + participio.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“Paul and Laura went back to home after finish the report.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Paul and Laura went back home after <u>finishing</u> the report.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: <em>go back home</em> no lleva <em>to</em> delante de <em>home</em>. Segundo error: detrás de la preposición <em>after</em> el verbo va en -ing.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘Did Ms. Warren take her watch to the jeweller’s yesterday?’ the porter said.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The porter asked if/whether Ms. Warren had taken her watch to the jeweller’s the day before / the previous day.</strong></p>
    <p>Una pregunta de sí o no se introduce con <em>if/whether</em>, sin <em>did</em> ni inversión. El pasado simple retrocede a pasado perfecto y <em>yesterday</em> pasa a <em>the day before</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>a</span><span>the</span><span>them</span><span>let</span><span>team</span><span>fight</span><span>without</span><span>opposing</span><span>win</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The opposing team let them win without a fight.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Let</em> + objeto va seguido de infinitivo sin <em>to</em> (<em>let them win</em>). <em>Opposing</em> es un adjetivo que va delante de <em>team</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
