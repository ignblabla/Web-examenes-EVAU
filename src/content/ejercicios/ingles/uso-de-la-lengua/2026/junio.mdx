<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points; 0.5 points each)</strong>. CHOOSE OPTION 1 OR OPTION 2 AND ANSWER <strong><u>ALL</u></strong> THE QUESTIONS FROM THAT OPTION <strong><u>ONLY</u></strong>.</p>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 1</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B1. I have never seen a more interesting documentary.</strong><br /><strong>That’s…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>That’s the most interesting documentary I have (ever) seen.</strong></p>
    <p>Del comparativo con <em>never</em> (<em>never… a more interesting</em>) se pasa al superlativo (<em>the most interesting</em>), sin cambiar el present perfect. <em>Ever</em> refuerza la idea de “en toda mi vida” y es habitual con el superlativo, pero no es obligatorio.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B2. Unlike my parents, who enjoy watching movies in the sitting room, I like to stream them in bed.</strong><br /><strong>Whereas…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Whereas my parents enjoy watching movies in the sitting room, I like to stream them in bed.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Unlike</em> es una preposición y va seguida de un nombre; <em>whereas</em> es una conjunción de contraste y necesita una oración completa con sujeto y verbo, por eso desaparece el relativo <em>who</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B3. My neighbour doesn’t like parties very much.</strong><br /><strong>(FOND)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>My neighbour is not (very) fond of parties.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Be fond of</em> + nombre o -ing significa “gustar, tener cariño a”. El verbo pasa a ser <em>be</em>, y <em>very much</em> se convierte en <em>very</em> delante del adjetivo. Sin <em>very</em> la frase también es correcta.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B4. Security knows the number of people inside the building at every time.</strong><br /><strong>(MANY)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Security knows how many people (there) are inside the building at every time.</strong></p>
    <p><em>The number of people</em> se sustituye por una interrogativa indirecta con <em>how many</em>. Al ser indirecta, lleva el orden de una afirmativa: <em>how many people there are</em>, no <em>how many people are there</em>. También se puede omitir <em>there</em>, porque <em>how many people</em> funciona como sujeto: <em>how many people are inside the building</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B5. Tim founded an advertising firm, but it has recently gone bankrupt.</strong><br /><strong>(WHICH)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Tim founded an advertising firm which has recently gone bankrupt.</strong></p>
    <p>Las dos oraciones coordinadas con <em>but</em> se unen en una sola con una oración de relativo: <em>which</em> sustituye al pronombre <em>it</em>, que se refiere a <em>an advertising firm</em>, y desaparece <em>but</em>. También es correcto escribirla como relativa explicativa, con coma: <em>…an advertising firm, which has recently gone bankrupt</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B6. Clara invited Nigel to dance.</strong><br /><strong>“Would you…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>“Would you like to dance (with me)?” Clara asked Nigel.</strong></p>
    <p>Se pasa de estilo indirecto a directo. Un verbo de invitación (<em>invite somebody to do something</em>) se convierte en la fórmula de ofrecimiento <em>Would you like to…?</em>, entre comillas y con signo de interrogación. Fuera de las comillas se indica quién habla y a quién: <em>Clara asked Nigel</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 2</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B7. It is known that some influencers buy their Instagram followers.</strong><br /><strong>Some influencers…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Some influencers are known to buy their Instagram followers.</strong></p>
    <p>Pasiva impersonal con sujeto personal: <em>It is known that + sujeto + verbo</em> → <em>Sujeto + is/are known + to-infinitivo</em>. Como la acción es habitual y simultánea, se usa el infinitivo simple <em>to buy</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B8. It’s a pity I can’t afford to travel abroad next summer.</strong><br /><strong>(WISH)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I wish I could (afford to) travel abroad next summer.</strong></p>
    <p>Para lamentar una situación presente o futura se usa <em>wish</em> + pasado. El presente negativo <em>can’t</em> pasa a <em>could</em> en afirmativa, porque el deseo es lo contrario de la realidad. <em>Afford to</em> se puede mantener o quitar: <em>I wish I could travel abroad</em> expresa la misma idea.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B9. Google Maps and some snacks are the only things you need for a good hike.</strong><br /><strong>All…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>All you need for a good hike is Google Maps and some snacks.</strong> (también <em>All that you need…</em>)</p>
    <p><em>All (that) you need</em> equivale a <em>the only thing(s) you need</em>. El sujeto pasa a ser <em>all</em>, que funciona como singular, por eso el verbo es <em>is</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B10. Someone is looking after the baby while the couple are out for the evening.</strong><br /><strong>(HAVING)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The couple are having the(ir) baby looked after while they are out for the evening.</strong></p>
    <p>Estructura causativa <em>have + objeto + participio</em>: la pareja no cuida al bebé, sino que encarga a otra persona que lo haga. Se mantiene el presente continuo (<em>are having</em>) y el agente (<em>someone</em>) desaparece. Vale tanto <em>the baby</em> como <em>their baby</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B11. I’m sure that Joe stayed up gaming all night, because he looks very tired.</strong><br /><strong>(MUST)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Joe must have stayed up gaming all night, because he looks very tired.</strong></p>
    <p>Para expresar una deducción casi segura sobre el pasado se usa <em>must have</em> + participio. Sustituye a <em>I’m sure that…</em> + pasado simple.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B12. You can reduce stress significantly by doing yoga.</strong><br /><strong>As long…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>As long as you do yoga, you can reduce stress significantly.</strong></p>
    <p><em>As long as</em> (“siempre que”) introduce una condición, igual que <em>if</em>. La forma <em>by doing</em> se convierte en una oración con sujeto y verbo conjugado: <em>you do yoga</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
