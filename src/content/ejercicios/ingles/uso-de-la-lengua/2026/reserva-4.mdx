<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points; 0.5 points each)</strong>. CHOOSE OPTION 1 OR OPTION 2 AND ANSWER <strong><u>ALL</u></strong> THE QUESTIONS FROM THAT OPTION <strong><u>ONLY</u></strong>.</p>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 1</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B1. I’ve never heard such a sad story before.</strong><br /><strong>That’s…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>That’s the saddest story I’ve ever heard.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Never… such a</em> + adjetivo se convierte en un superlativo con <em>ever</em>: <em>the saddest… I’ve ever heard</em>. Se mantiene el present perfect.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B2. If they want to attract more visitors, tickets should be cheaper.</strong><br /><strong>In order…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>In order to attract more visitors, they should make tickets cheaper.</strong> (también <em>In order to attract more visitors, tickets should be cheaper.</em>)</p>
    <p>La condición con <em>if they want to…</em> expresa en realidad una finalidad, así que se usa <em>in order to</em> + infinitivo. Lo más correcto es que el sujeto de la oración principal sea el mismo que el de la finalidad (<em>they</em>), porque son ellos, no las entradas, quienes quieren atraer visitantes.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B3. It is forbidden to drink alcohol on campus.</strong><br /><strong>You…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You mustn’t (o aren’t allowed to / can’t) drink alcohol on campus.</strong></p>
    <p>La prohibición impersonal (<em>it is forbidden to</em>) se expresa con el sujeto genérico <em>you</em> y un modal de prohibición: <em>mustn’t</em> o <em>can’t</em> + infinitivo sin <em>to</em>, o <em>be not allowed to</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B4. Paul didn’t think about his options clearly, so he didn’t make the right decision.</strong><br /><strong>If…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If Paul had thought about his options clearly, he would have made the right decision.</strong></p>
    <p>Tercer condicional, sobre una situación pasada que no ocurrió: <em>if</em> + pasado perfecto, <em>would have</em> + participio. Las dos oraciones negativas pasan a afirmativas porque se imagina lo contrario de lo que pasó.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B5. We don’t have any flour left for the muffins.</strong><br /><strong>(RUN)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>We have run out of flour for the muffins.</strong></p>
    <p>El <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>run out of</em> significa “quedarse sin algo”. Se usa el present perfect porque la consecuencia es presente: ahora no queda harina.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B6. I don’t like movies as much as I like TV series.</strong><br /><strong>(PREFER)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I prefer TV series to movies.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Prefer A to B</em> significa “preferir A a B”: lo que gusta más va detrás de <em>prefer</em> y lo que gusta menos, detrás de <em>to</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 2</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B7. I last saw Alice two weeks ago.</strong><br /><strong>(FOR)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I haven’t seen Alice for two weeks.</strong></p>
    <p><em>I last saw… + periodo + ago</em> equivale al present perfect negativo con <em>for</em> + periodo: hace dos semanas que no la veo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B8. María Dueñas is said to be the greatest violinist at the time.</strong><br /><strong>People…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>People say (that) María Dueñas is the greatest violinist at the time.</strong></p>
    <p>Se pasa de la pasiva con sujeto personal (<em>is said to be</em>) a la activa con el sujeto genérico <em>people</em>: <em>people say (that)</em> + oración con el verbo conjugado (<em>is</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B9. Both hoodies were unsuitable for Martin.</strong><br /><strong>Neither...</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Neither hoodie was suitable for Martin.</strong> (o <strong>Neither of the hoodies was/were suitable for Martin.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Neither</em> ya es negativo, así que el adjetivo pasa a su forma afirmativa (<em>unsuitable</em> → <em>suitable</em>). <em>Neither</em> va con un nombre en singular y verbo en singular; con <em>neither of the</em> + plural, el verbo puede ir en singular o en plural.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B10. At the interview, they were asked the date of their wedding anniversary.</strong><br /><strong>(MARRIED)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>At the interview, they were asked when they (had) got married.</strong></p>
    <p>Preguntar por la fecha del aniversario de boda es preguntar cuándo se casaron. Con <em>get married</em> se forma una pregunta indirecta con <em>when</em>, en orden de afirmativa (sujeto + verbo) y en pasado o pasado perfecto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B11. I will send you the location, and you must wait for him there.</strong><br /><strong>(WHERE)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I will send you the location where you must wait for him.</strong></p>
    <p>El adverbio <em>there</em> se refiere a <em>the location</em>, así que se sustituye por el relativo de lugar <em>where</em>, y desaparece <em>and</em>. Es una relativa especificativa, sin comas.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B12. Could you help me find a smart outfit for my graduation ceremony?</strong><br /><strong>(MIND)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Would you mind helping me find a smart outfit for my graduation ceremony?</strong></p>
    <p>Una petición educada con <em>Could you…?</em> se puede expresar con <em>Would you mind</em> + -ing (“¿te importaría…?”).</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
