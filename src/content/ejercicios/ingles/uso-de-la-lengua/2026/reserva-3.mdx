<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points; 0.5 points each)</strong>. CHOOSE OPTION 1 OR OPTION 2 AND ANSWER <strong><u>ALL</u></strong> THE QUESTIONS FROM THAT OPTION <strong><u>ONLY</u></strong>.</p>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 1</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B1. Scientists claimed that the new treatment had saved lots of lives.</strong><br /><strong>The new treatment…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The new treatment was claimed to have saved lots of lives.</strong></p>
    <p>Pasiva con sujeto personal: <em>Sujeto + was claimed + to</em>-infinitivo. Como salvar vidas es anterior a la afirmación, se usa el infinitivo perfecto <em>to have saved</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B2. I am not sure whether that man is Andrew’s father.</strong><br /><strong>(MIGHT)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>That man might (not) be Andrew’s father.</strong></p>
    <p>La duda en presente se expresa con el modal de posibilidad <em>might</em> + infinitivo sin <em>to</em>. Vale en afirmativa o en negativa, porque la duda incluye las dos posibilidades.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B3. The loud thunder frightened the children.</strong><br /><strong>(AFRAID)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The children were afraid of the loud thunder.</strong></p>
    <p>El verbo <em>frighten</em> se sustituye por el adjetivo <em>afraid</em>, que va con <em>be</em> y la preposición <em>of</em>. Los niños pasan a ser el sujeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B4. Louise doesn’t eat meat anymore, and neither does Frank.</strong><br /><strong>(NOR)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Neither Louise nor Frank eats meat anymore.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Neither… nor</em> (“ni… ni”) une los dos sujetos, y el verbo va en afirmativa porque la negación ya está en <em>neither… nor</em>. El verbo concuerda con el sujeto más cercano, <em>Frank</em>: <em>eats</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B5. She will probably be tired after the long flight.</strong><br /><strong>(LIKELY)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>She’s likely to be tired after the long flight.</strong> (o <strong>It is likely (that) she will be tired after the long flight.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Probably</em> se expresa con <em>be likely to</em> + infinitivo, o con la forma impersonal <em>it is likely (that)</em> + oración.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B6. It’s too warm outside to wear my winter coat.</strong><br /><strong>It is not…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>It is not cold enough outside to wear my winter coat.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Too</em> + adjetivo equivale a <em>not</em> + adjetivo contrario + <em>enough</em>: <em>too warm</em> → <em>not cold enough</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B7. “I’m sorry I spilled the expensive shampoo,” David said.</strong><br /><strong>(APOLOGIZED)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>David apologized for spilling / having spilled the expensive shampoo.</strong> (o <strong>David apologized (that) he (had) spilled/spilt the expensive shampoo.</strong>)</p>
    <p>En estilo indirecto, <em>I’m sorry</em> se resume con <em>apologize for</em> + -ing (o gerundio perfecto). También vale <em>apologize (that)</em> + oración, con el verbo en pasado o pasado perfecto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B8. My brother hasn’t gone skydiving for years.</strong><br /><strong>It’s been years…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>It’s been years since my brother (last) went skydiving.</strong></p>
    <p>El present perfect negativo con <em>for</em> equivale a <em>it’s been</em> + periodo + <em>since</em> + pasado simple, en afirmativa.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B9. The government is going to send us a message alerting about the critical situation.</strong><br /><strong>We…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>We are going to be sent a message alerting about the critical situation (by the government).</strong></p>
    <p><em>Send</em> tiene dos objetos; aquí la persona (<em>us</em> → <em>we</em>) pasa a ser el sujeto. Con <em>be going to</em> la pasiva se forma con <em>going to be</em> + participio.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B10. I purchased my scooter from that seller.</strong><br /><strong>(WHOM)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>That’s the seller from whom I purchased my scooter.</strong> (o <strong>That’s the seller whom I purchased my scooter from.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Whom</em> es el relativo de persona en función de objeto. La preposición <em>from</em> puede ir delante de <em>whom</em> o al final de la oración, pero no en los dos sitios a la vez.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B11. Whose smartwatch is this?</strong><br /><strong>(BELONG)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Who does this smartwatch belong to?</strong></p>
    <p>El poseedor se pregunta con <em>belong to</em> + <em>who</em>. La pregunta en presente simple lleva <em>does</em>, y la preposición <em>to</em> queda al final.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B12. Let’s order some Chinese food tonight.</strong><br /><strong>How…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How about (ordering) some Chinese food tonight?</strong></p>
    <p>Una sugerencia con <em>Let’s</em> + infinitivo se puede expresar con <em>How about</em> + -ing o + nombre. Termina con signo de interrogación.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
