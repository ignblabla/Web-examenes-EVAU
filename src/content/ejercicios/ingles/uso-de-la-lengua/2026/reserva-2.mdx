<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points; 0.5 points each)</strong>. CHOOSE OPTION 1 OR OPTION 2 AND ANSWER <strong><u>ALL</u></strong> THE QUESTIONS FROM THAT OPTION <strong><u>ONLY</u></strong>.</p>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 1</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B1. I watched the first season of Stranger Things faster than the second.</strong><br /><strong>(AS)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I didn’t watch the second season of Stranger Things as fast as (I did/watched) the first (one).</strong></p>
    <p>El comparativo de superioridad (<em>faster than</em>) se convierte en comparativo de igualdad negativo (<em>not as fast as</em>), invirtiendo los términos: la segunda temporada no la vi tan rápido como la primera.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B2. “Be careful on the slippery floor,” the janitor said to the students.</strong><br /><strong>(WARNED)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The janitor warned the students to be careful on the slippery floor.</strong> (o <strong>The janitor warned the students about the slippery floor.</strong>)</p>
    <p>En estilo indirecto, una advertencia se construye con <em>warn</em> + persona + <em>to</em>-infinitivo, o <em>warn</em> + persona + <em>about</em> + nombre.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B3. Despite not feeling like going out that day, Jason met his mates at the pub.</strong><br /><strong>Although…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Although Jason didn’t feel like going out that day, he met his mates at the pub.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Despite</em> va seguido de -ing o de un nombre; <em>although</em> necesita una oración completa con sujeto y verbo conjugado: <em>Jason didn’t feel like</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B4. I advise you not to use your phone before going to bed at night.</strong><br /><strong>(BETTER)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You’d better not use your phone before going to bed at night / before bedtime.</strong></p>
    <p>Un consejo se expresa con <em>had better (not)</em> + infinitivo sin <em>to</em>. La negación va detrás de <em>better</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B5. Sarah really enjoys sitting outside and sunbathing.</strong><br /><strong>(KEEN)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Sarah is really keen on sitting outside and sunbathing.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Be keen on</em> + -ing (“ser muy aficionado a”) equivale a <em>enjoy</em> + -ing. Lleva el verbo <em>be</em> y la preposición <em>on</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B6. A friend who knows a lot about computers has fixed my laptop.</strong><br /><strong>I’ve…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I’ve had/got(ten) my laptop fixed by a friend who knows a lot about computers.</strong></p>
    <p>Estructura causativa <em>have/get</em> + objeto + participio: yo encargué el arreglo y lo hizo otra persona. Se mantiene el present perfect y el agente va con <em>by</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 2</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B7. All participants in the contest have to sign this form.</strong><br /><strong>Every…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Every participant in the contest has to sign this form.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Every</em> va con un nombre en singular, así que el verbo también pasa a singular: <em>participants have</em> → <em>participant has</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B8. The team will have completed the new climate report by the end of the month.</strong><br /><strong>The new climate report…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The new climate report will have been completed (by the team) by the end of the month.</strong></p>
    <p>Pasiva del futuro perfecto: <em>will have been</em> + participio. El agente (<em>by the team</em>) puede mantenerse u omitirse.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B9. She has enough confidence to speak in public.</strong><br /><strong>She is…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>She is confident enough to speak in public.</strong></p>
    <p>El nombre <em>confidence</em> pasa al adjetivo <em>confident</em>. <em>Enough</em> va delante de un nombre (<em>enough confidence</em>), pero detrás de un adjetivo (<em>confident enough</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B10. Everybody must enter the lab with an identification badge.</strong><br /><strong>Nobody…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Nobody is allowed to (o is to / can / may) enter the lab without an identification badge.</strong></p>
    <p>Si todos deben entrar con la tarjeta, nadie puede entrar sin ella: <em>nobody</em> + permiso (<em>is allowed to</em>, <em>can</em>, <em>may</em>) y <em>with</em> se convierte en <em>without</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B11. The match was cancelled due to the heavy rain.</strong><br /><strong>(BECAUSE)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The match was cancelled because it rained / was raining heavily.</strong> (o <strong>The match was cancelled because of the heavy rain.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Because</em> va seguido de una oración con sujeto y verbo (el adjetivo <em>heavy</em> pasa al adverbio <em>heavily</em>), y <em>because of</em> va seguido de un nombre, igual que <em>due to</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B12. She must train hard to win the swimming competition.</strong><br /><strong>If…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If she doesn’t train hard, she won’t win the swimming competition.</strong></p>
    <p>La obligación se expresa como un primer condicional negativo: <em>if</em> + presente simple, <em>will(n’t)</em> + infinitivo. Si no entrena, no ganará.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
