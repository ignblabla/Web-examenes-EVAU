<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points; 0.5 points each)</strong>. CHOOSE OPTION 1 OR OPTION 2 AND ANSWER <strong><u>ALL</u></strong> THE QUESTIONS FROM THAT OPTION <strong><u>ONLY</u></strong>.</p>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 1</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B1. I wish we had access to streaming platforms at the hotel.</strong><br /><strong>(PITY)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>It’s a pity (that) we don’t have access to streaming platforms at the hotel.</strong></p>
    <p><em>I wish</em> + pasado lamenta una situación presente. Con <em>It’s a pity (that)</em> se describe la realidad, así que el verbo vuelve al presente y pasa a negativa: <em>we don’t have</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B2. He called a moment ago to cancel the appointment.</strong><br /><strong>He has…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>He has just called to cancel the appointment.</strong></p>
    <p>Para una acción que acaba de ocurrir se usa el present perfect con <em>just</em>, que sustituye a <em>a moment ago</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B3. “Why did they cancel Kendrick Lamar’s concert yesterday?” he asked.</strong><br /><strong>He asked why…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>He asked why they had canceled Kendrick Lamar’s concert the day before / the previous day.</strong> (o <strong>He asked why Kendrick Lamar’s concert had been canceled the day before / the previous day.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Pregunta en estilo indirecto: el pasado simple retrocede a pasado perfecto, desaparece el auxiliar <em>did</em> y no hay inversión. <em>Yesterday</em> pasa a <em>the day before</em> o <em>the previous day</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B4. The whole school is talking about our viral video, but it got deleted for no reason.</strong><br /><strong>Our viral video, about…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Our viral video, about which the whole school is talking, got deleted for no reason.</strong></p>
    <p>Relativa explicativa entre comas. Como empieza por la preposición <em>about</em>, el relativo tiene que ser <em>which</em> (no <em>that</em>), y desaparecen <em>but</em> e <em>it</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B5. They had spent years avoiding the issue, but they finally talked about what had happened.</strong><br /><strong>After years…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>After years (of) avoiding the issue, they finally talked about what had happened.</strong></p>
    <p>La primera oración se reduce a un complemento con <em>after</em>: <em>after years (of)</em> + -ing. Desaparecen <em>had spent</em> y <em>but</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B6. I look forward to visiting Tokyo.</strong><br /><strong>(WAIT)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I can’t wait to visit Tokyo.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Can’t wait</em> + <em>to</em>-infinitivo (“estar deseando”) equivale a <em>look forward to</em> + -ing.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 2</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B7. I didn’t have an umbrella with me, so I got soaked when it started to rain.</strong><br /><strong>I wouldn’t…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I wouldn’t have got(ten) soaked (when it started to rain) if I had had an umbrella with me.</strong></p>
    <p>Tercer condicional, sobre una situación pasada que no ocurrió: <em>would(n’t) have</em> + participio en la principal e <em>if</em> + pasado perfecto en la condición. Se imagina lo contrario de lo que pasó: <em>didn’t have</em> → <em>had had</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B8. I can’t speak Italian as well as Sue.</strong><br /><strong>Sue can…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Sue can speak Italian better than me / I do.</strong></p>
    <p>El comparativo de igualdad negativo se convierte en comparativo de superioridad invirtiendo los términos. <em>Better</em> es el comparativo irregular de <em>well</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B9. You’d better not abuse fast food.</strong><br /><strong>(OUGHT)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You ought not to abuse fast food.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Ought to</em> expresa un consejo, como <em>had better</em>. En negativa, <em>not</em> va entre <em>ought</em> y <em>to</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B10. Nobody warned her about the consequences.</strong><br /><strong>She…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>She was not warned about the consequences.</strong></p>
    <p>Pasiva en pasado simple: el objeto <em>her</em> pasa a ser el sujeto <em>she</em>. Como el sujeto <em>nobody</em> era negativo, la pasiva va en negativa y el agente desaparece.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B11. You still haven’t updated your phone.</strong><br /><strong>It’s time…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>It’s time you updated your phone.</strong> (o <strong>It’s time (for you) to update your phone.</strong> / <strong>It’s time for a phone update.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>It’s time</em> + sujeto + pasado simple indica que algo ya debería haberse hecho; el pasado se refiere al presente. También vale <em>it’s time (for you) to</em> + infinitivo o <em>it’s time for</em> + nombre.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B12. The firefighters ran out of the warehouse so that they wouldn’t get trapped.</strong><br /><strong>So as not…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>So as not to get trapped, the firefighters ran out of the warehouse.</strong></p>
    <p>La finalidad negativa con <em>so that… wouldn’t</em> se expresa con <em>so as not to</em> + infinitivo, que no lleva sujeto propio porque coincide con el de la oración principal.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
