<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“As my first New Year’s resolution, I intend… Chinese.”</strong> (learn)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“I remember… very nervous on my first job interview.”</strong> (be)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “As my first New Year’s resolution, I intend <strong>to learn</strong> Chinese.”</p>
    <p>El verbo <em>intend</em> (“tener intención de”) va seguido de <em>to</em> + infinitivo.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “I remember <strong>being</strong> (o <strong>having been</strong>) very nervous on my first job interview.”</p>
    <p><em>Remember</em> + -ing se usa para recordar algo que ya pasó. También vale el gerundio perfecto, <em>having been</em>, que subraya que es anterior. No hay que confundirlo con <em>remember to</em> + infinitivo, que significa “acordarse de hacer algo”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“The shopping centre is… the hospital and the football stadium.”</strong> at / among / between / close</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“My mother… her boyfriend at a singles party last August.”</strong> met / acquainted / realised / knew</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “The shopping centre is <strong>between</strong> the hospital and the football stadium.”</p>
    <p><em>Between</em> (“entre”) se usa con dos elementos (<em>between… and…</em>); <em>among</em> se usa con más de dos. <em>Close</em> necesitaría <em>to</em>.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “My mother <strong>met</strong> her boyfriend at a singles party last August.”</p>
    <p><em>Meet</em> significa “conocer a alguien por primera vez”. <em>Know</em> expresa un estado (“conocer” en el sentido de tener trato), <em>realise</em> es “darse cuenta” y <em>acquainted</em> necesitaría <em>got acquainted with</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“The parcel is expected to reach its destination before the wedding.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They/We expect (that) the parcel reaches/will reach its destination before the wedding.</strong></p>
    <p>Es una pasiva con sujeto personal (<em>The parcel is expected to…</em>). En activa se usa un sujeto genérico (<em>they</em> o <em>we</em>) con <em>expect</em> y una oración con <em>that</em>, que se puede omitir. El infinitivo <em>to reach</em> pasa a verbo conjugado: <em>reaches</em> o <em>will reach</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“The girl from Syria has taught the students the best lesson in their lives.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The students have been taught the best lesson in their lives by the girl from Syria.</strong> (o <strong>The best lesson in their lives has been taught to the students by the girl from Syria.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Teach</em> tiene dos objetos, así que cualquiera de los dos puede ser el sujeto de la pasiva. Se mantiene el present perfect (<em>has/have been taught</em>), y si el sujeto es la lección, la persona va con <em>to</em>. El agente va con <em>by</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“My students are going to borrow the book from <u>the school library</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Where are your/my students going to borrow the book (from)?</strong></p>
    <p>Se pregunta por un lugar con <em>where</em>. Con <em>be going to</em>, el verbo <em>are</em> va delante del sujeto. La preposición <em>from</em> puede quedarse al final.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“Many Erasmus students choose Greece. Greece is known as the cradle of western civilization.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Many Erasmus students choose Greece, which is known as the cradle of western civilization.</strong> (o <strong>Greece, which is known as the cradle of western civilization, is chosen by many Erasmus students.</strong>)</p>
    <p>El segundo <em>Greece</em> se sustituye por el relativo <em>which</em>. Como es un nombre propio, la relativa es explicativa y va entre comas. En la segunda opción la oración principal pasa a pasiva para que <em>Greece</em> sea el sujeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“It was a mistake for us to leave.”</strong> We shouldn’t...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>We shouldn’t have left.</strong></p>
    <p>Para expresar que algo que se hizo en el pasado fue un error se usa <em>shouldn’t have</em> + participio.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“I would have followed the path if...”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I would have followed the path if I had seen the signs.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Es un tercer condicional, sobre una situación pasada que no ocurrió: si la oración principal lleva <em>would have</em> + participio, la condición va con <em>if</em> + sujeto + pasado perfecto. Cualquier condición con sentido y con esa estructura es correcta.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“When did you buy your tablet?”</strong> How long...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How long have you had your tablet?</strong></p>
    <p>Con <em>how long</em> se pregunta por una duración que llega hasta el presente, así que se usa el present perfect. <em>Buy</em> es una acción puntual; se sustituye por <em>have</em> (“tener”), que expresa un estado que dura: <em>have had</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“They use to get in well with their relatives.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They <u>used</u> to get <u>on</u> well with their relatives.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: para un hábito pasado se usa <em>used to</em> + infinitivo, no <em>use to</em>. Segundo error: “llevarse bien con alguien” es el <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>get on well with</em>, no <em>get in</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘Don’t leave your luggage unattended,’ a member of the aircrew told us.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>A member of the aircrew told/advised us not to leave the luggage unattended.</strong> (o <strong>A member of the aircrew suggested (that) we (should) not leave the luggage unattended.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Una orden o consejo negativo en estilo indirecto se construye con <em>tell/advise</em> + persona + <em>not to</em> + infinitivo, o con <em>suggest (that)</em> + oración. El posesivo <em>your</em> se refiere a nosotros, así que cambia a <em>our</em> o simplemente <em>the</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>no</span><span>pleasant</span><span>have</span><span>to</span><span>how</span><span>you</span><span>swim</span><span>is</span><span>here</span><span>it</span><span>idea</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You have no idea how pleasant it is to swim here.</strong></p>
    <p>La expresión <em>have no idea</em> (“no tener ni idea”) va seguida de una interrogativa indirecta con <em>how</em> + adjetivo, en orden de afirmativa: <em>how pleasant it is</em>. <em>It</em> anticipa el sujeto real, <em>to swim here</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
