<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“I feel a lot healthier after… smoking.”</strong> (quit)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“Last summer we… to the Canary Islands on holidays.”</strong> (travel)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “I feel a lot healthier after <strong>quitting</strong> smoking.”</p>
    <p>Detrás de la preposición <em>after</em> el verbo va en -ing (con doble <em>t</em>: <em>quitting</em>).</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “Last summer we <strong>travelled</strong> (o <strong>traveled</strong>) to the Canary Islands on holidays.”</p>
    <p>Con <em>last summer</em> se usa el pasado simple.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“The convict did not admit… the crime.”</strong> to commit / to committing / commit / have committed</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“Young people are usually willing… new experiences.”</strong> about living / to live / with live / living</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “The convict did not admit <strong>to committing</strong> the crime.”</p>
    <p><em>Admit (to)</em> va seguido de -ing; aquí <em>to</em> es una preposición.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “Young people are usually willing <strong>to live</strong> new experiences.”</p>
    <p><em>Be willing to</em> + infinitivo significa “estar dispuesto a”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“The actor was given an award for his amazing performance.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They (o The Academy) gave the actor an award for his amazing performance.</strong></p>
    <p>Como la pasiva no indica quién dio el premio, se usa <em>they</em> o el agente lógico. <em>Give</em> tiene dos objetos: la persona va delante sin preposición.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“The government is announcing new aids for charitable organizations tomorrow.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>(Tomorrow) New aids for charitable organizations are being announced by the government (tomorrow).</strong></p>
    <p>Pasiva del presente continuo (con valor de futuro): <em>are being</em> + participio, en plural porque el sujeto es <em>new aids</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“In Japan, a black cat crossing your path means <u>good luck</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>What does a black cat crossing your path in Japan mean?</strong> (o <strong>…mean in Japan?</strong>)</p>
    <p>Se pregunta por el objeto con <em>what</em>, con el auxiliar <em>does</em> y el verbo en infinitivo (<em>mean</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“My eldest sister won the match. She was playing against a foreign competitor.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>My eldest sister, who was playing against a foreign competitor, won the match.</strong></p>
    <p>El pronombre <em>she</em> se sustituye por <em>who</em> en una relativa explicativa entre comas.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“Your neighbours aren’t as nice as mine.”</strong> My neighbours are…</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>My neighbours are nicer than yours.</strong></p>
    <p>El comparativo de igualdad negativo se convierte en comparativo de superioridad invirtiendo los términos.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“If I could fly, …”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If I could fly, I would travel all over the world.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Segundo condicional (situación imaginaria): la principal lleva sujeto + <em>would/could (not)</em> + infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“Taking care of three little kids can be exhausting.”</strong> It...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>It can be exhausting to take care of three little kids.</strong></p>
    <p>El sujeto en -ing se sustituye por <em>it</em> al principio, y la acción pasa al final con <em>to</em>-infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“He can go out with you these weekend because he hasn’t to look after his brothers.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>He can go out with you <u>this</u> weekend because he <u>doesn’t have to</u> look after his brothers.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: <em>weekend</em> es singular, así que el demostrativo es <em>this</em>. Segundo error: la negativa de <em>have to</em> se forma con el auxiliar <em>doesn’t</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO DIRECT SPEECH: <strong>“She asked me whether I had practised any sport in my free time when I was younger.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>She asked me, “Did you practise any sport in your free time when you were younger?”</strong></p>
    <p>En estilo directo la pregunta recupera el orden interrogativo con <em>did</em>; el pasado perfecto vuelve a pasado simple y <em>I/my</em> pasan a <em>you/your</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>listened</span><span>advice</span><span>if</span><span>to</span><span>her</span><span>only</span><span>had</span><span>he</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If only he had listened to her advice.</strong></p>
    <p><em>If only</em> + pasado perfecto expresa un lamento sobre el pasado (“ojalá hubiera…”).</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
