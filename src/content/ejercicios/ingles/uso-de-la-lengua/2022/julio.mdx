<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“He always makes me… when I feel sad.”</strong> (smile)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“They… since they bought the phone. I think they will finally get the refund.”</strong> (complain)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “He always makes me <strong>smile</strong> when I feel sad.”</p>
    <p><em>Make</em> + objeto (“hacer que alguien haga algo”) va seguido de infinitivo sin <em>to</em>.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “They <strong>have complained</strong> (o <strong>have been complaining</strong>) since they bought the phone. I think they will finally get the refund.”</p>
    <p>Con <em>since</em> + momento del pasado se usa el present perfect, para una acción que empezó en el pasado y sigue hasta ahora. La forma continua (<em>have been complaining</em>) subraya que la acción se ha repetido o prolongado.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“Frankly, there’s no reason to be mad… this. Don’t get upset, please.”</strong> of / from / about / behind</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“After using the spray, there were still… ants in the kitchen.”</strong> much / little / a little / a few</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “Frankly, there’s no reason to be mad <strong>about</strong> this. Don’t get upset, please.”</p>
    <p>Para expresar el motivo del enfado se usa <em>be mad about something</em>; con la persona se usa <em>at</em>.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “After using the spray, there were still <strong>a few</strong> ants in the kitchen.”</p>
    <p><em>Ants</em> es contable y plural, así que se descartan <em>much</em>, <em>little</em> y <em>a little</em>, que son para incontables. <em>A few</em> significa “algunas”: con <em>still</em> indica que aún quedaban unas cuantas.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“The passengers were told to stay away from the bus until the door opened.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They/The driver told the passengers to stay away from the bus until the door opened.</strong></p>
    <p>Como la pasiva no indica quién dio la orden, en activa se usa un sujeto genérico (<em>they</em>) o el agente lógico (<em>the driver</em>). Se mantiene el pasado simple, <em>were told</em> → <em>told</em>, y <em>the passengers</em> pasa a ser el objeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“The staff at the shopping centre is already working on next year’s campaign.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Next year’s campaign is already being worked on by the staff at the shopping centre.</strong></p>
    <p>El objeto del verbo preposicional <em>work on</em> pasa a ser el sujeto, y la preposición <em>on</em> se queda detrás del participio. Se mantiene el presente continuo: <em>is being worked on</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“Martina criticised the organization of the event <u>a lot</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How much (o How often / How strongly) did Martina criticise the organization of the event?</strong></p>
    <p><em>A lot</em> indica cantidad o intensidad, así que se pregunta con <em>how much</em>, <em>how often</em> o <em>how strongly</em>. Como el verbo está en pasado simple, la pregunta lleva <em>did</em> y el verbo en infinitivo: <em>criticised</em> → <em>did… criticise</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“Randy’s pet needs to be kept in a cage. It is a rather dangerous snake.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Randy’s pet, which is a rather dangerous snake, needs to be kept in a cage.</strong></p>
    <p>El pronombre <em>it</em> se sustituye por el relativo <em>which</em>. Es una relativa explicativa, porque solo añade información sobre una mascota ya identificada, así que va entre comas.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“If I were you, I wouldn’t tweet them any more messages like this one.”</strong> You’d better…</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You’d better not tweet them any more messages like this one.</strong> (o <strong>You’d better stop tweeting them any more messages like this one.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Un consejo con <em>If I were you, I wouldn’t…</em> se expresa con <em>had better not</em> + infinitivo sin <em>to</em>. También vale <em>had better stop</em> + -ing.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“As long as the doctor calls, …”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>As long as the doctor calls, I will stay at home.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p><em>As long as</em> (“siempre que”) introduce una condición en presente, como un primer condicional. La oración principal lleva sujeto + <em>will/can/may (not)</em> + infinitivo, o un imperativo (<em>…, let me know</em>). Cualquier final con sentido y con esa estructura es correcto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“It has been over two decades since The Matrix revolutionised the science fiction genre.”</strong> The Matrix…</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The Matrix revolutionised the science-fiction genre over two decades ago.</strong></p>
    <p><em>It has been + periodo + since</em> + pasado equivale a pasado simple + periodo + <em>ago</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“My grandchildrens asked me accompanying them home.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>My <u>grandchildren</u> asked me <u>to accompany</u> them home.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: <em>grandchildren</em> ya es plural (irregular, como <em>children</em>), así que no lleva <em>-s</em>. Segundo error: <em>ask</em> + persona va seguido de <em>to</em> + infinitivo, no de -ing.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘I think I am going to update my Facebook profile tomorrow,’ Brian replied.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Brian replied that he thought he was going to update his Facebook profile the following day / the next day / the day after.</strong></p>
    <p>En estilo indirecto los tiempos retroceden (<em>think</em> → <em>thought</em>, <em>am going to</em> → <em>was going to</em>), cambian los pronombres (<em>I</em> → <em>he</em>, <em>my</em> → <em>his</em>) y la expresión de tiempo (<em>tomorrow</em> → <em>the following day</em>, <em>the next day</em> o <em>the day after</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>actors</span><span>is</span><span>googled</span><span>time</span><span>the</span><span>photo</span><span>of</span><span>about</span><span>most</span><span>all</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The most googled photo of all time is about actors.</strong></p>
    <p>El sujeto es un superlativo, <em>the most googled photo</em> (“la foto más buscada en Google”), seguido de la expresión fija <em>of all time</em> (“de todos los tiempos”). Después va el verbo <em>is</em> y <em>about actors</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
