<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“He threatened the other driver with… the police.”</strong> (call)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“Students… to stand up without permission.”</strong> (not allow)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “He threatened the other driver with <strong>calling</strong> the police.”</p>
    <p>Detrás de la preposición <em>with</em> el verbo va en -ing.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “Students <strong>aren’t allowed</strong> to stand up without permission.”</p>
    <p>La prohibición se expresa con la pasiva <em>be (not) allowed to</em> + infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“Why don’t you try… dishes?”</strong> another / others / the others / the other</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“The new coffee shop doesn’t appear… the map.”</strong> on / in / at / by</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “Why don’t you try <strong>the other</strong> dishes?”</p>
    <p>Delante de un nombre plural se usa <em>the other</em>, que no lleva <em>-s</em> porque es adjetivo; <em>others</em> es pronombre y <em>another</em> va con singular.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “The new coffee shop doesn’t appear <strong>on</strong> the map.”</p>
    <p>Se dice <em>on the map</em> (“en el mapa”).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“The new law will be passed by the end of the term.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They will pass the new law by the end of the term.</strong></p>
    <p>Se usa el sujeto genérico <em>they</em> y se mantiene el futuro: <em>will be passed</em> → <em>will pass</em>. Aquí <em>by</em> significa “para” (plazo), no introduce el agente.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“They saw her stealing a bracelet and arrested her.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>She was seen stealing a bracelet and (was) arrested.</strong></p>
    <p>Las dos acciones pasan a pasado simple pasivo con <em>she</em> como sujeto; el segundo <em>was</em> puede omitirse. <em>See somebody doing</em> mantiene el -ing en pasiva.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“The alarm was going off <u>every five minutes</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How often was the alarm going off?</strong></p>
    <p>Para preguntar por la frecuencia se usa <em>how often</em>, con el auxiliar <em>was</em> delante del sujeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“I would love to come back to Venice in August. We fell in love that month.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I would love to come back to Venice in August, when we fell in love.</strong> (o <strong>…, the month in which we fell in love.</strong> / <strong>…, the month (that/which) we fell in love in.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>That month</em> se refiere a un tiempo, así que se sustituye por <em>when</em>, o por <em>in which</em> con <em>the month</em> como antecedente.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“Pamela does a lot of exercise so as to become a firefighter.”</strong> Pamela does a lot of exercise because...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Pamela does a lot of exercise because she wants to become a firefighter.</strong></p>
    <p>La finalidad (<em>so as to</em> + infinitivo) se expresa como causa: <em>because</em> + oración con sujeto y verbo (<em>she wants to</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“Don’t take your umbrella if...”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Don’t take your umbrella if it isn’t raining.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Con un imperativo en la principal, la condición va con <em>if</em> + sujeto + presente simple o presente continuo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“How much does this green jacket cost, please?”</strong> Would you mind...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Would you mind telling me how much this green jacket costs (please)?</strong></p>
    <p><em>Would you mind</em> + -ing introduce una petición educada; la pregunta pasa a indirecta, sin <em>does</em> y con el verbo conjugado (<em>costs</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“There aren’t much people who rely to James.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>There aren’t <u>many</u> people who rely <u>on</u> James.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: <em>people</em> es contable y plural, así que va con <em>many</em>. Segundo error: <em>rely</em> lleva la preposición <em>on</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘Please, fill in the form before you leave,’ said the receptionist to the customer.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The receptionist asked (o requested) the customer to fill in the form before she/he left.</strong></p>
    <p>Una petición en estilo indirecto se construye con <em>ask/request</em> + persona + <em>to</em> + infinitivo; <em>leave</em> retrocede a <em>left</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>only</span><span>time</span><span>would</span><span>if</span><span>the</span><span>not</span><span>Robert</span><span>gum</span><span>all</span><span>chew</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If only Robert would not chew gum all the time.</strong></p>
    <p><em>If only</em> + <em>would (not)</em> + infinitivo expresa queja por una costumbre de otra persona que nos molesta.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
