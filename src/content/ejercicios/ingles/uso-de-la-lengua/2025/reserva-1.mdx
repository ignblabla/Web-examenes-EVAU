<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points; 0.5 points each)</strong>. CHOOSE OPTION 1 OR OPTION 2 AND ANSWER <strong><u>ALL</u></strong> THE QUESTIONS FROM THAT OPTION <strong><u>ONLY</u></strong>.</p>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 1</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets en-bullets--disc">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B1. What a pity you couldn’t come along to the theatre yesterday.</strong><br /><strong>(WISH)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I wish you had (been able to) come along to the theatre yesterday.</strong> (o <strong>I wish you could have come along to the theatre yesterday.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Para lamentar algo del pasado se usa <em>wish</em> + pasado perfecto. Como <em>could</em> no tiene pasado perfecto, se puede usar <em>had been able to</em> o <em>could have</em> + participio. La oración pasa a afirmativa porque se desea lo contrario de lo que ocurrió.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B2. Elena didn’t have a driving license, so she couldn’t get the job.</strong><br /><strong>If Elena…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If Elena had (had) a driving license, she would have got / would have been able to get the job.</strong></p>
    <p>Tercer condicional, para una situación pasada que no ocurrió: <em>if</em> + pasado perfecto (<em>had had</em>), <em>would have</em> + participio. Las dos oraciones pasan a afirmativas.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B3. Steven left early because he needed to pick up his son from nursery school.</strong><br /><strong>In order to…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>In order to pick up his son from nursery school, Steven left early.</strong></p>
    <p>La causa (<em>because he needed to…</em>) se expresa como finalidad con <em>in order to</em> + infinitivo. Al empezar la oración con la finalidad, se separa con una coma.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B4. It is possible that we will go to Isla Mágica tomorrow.</strong><br /><strong>We…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>We may / might / could go to Isla Mágica tomorrow.</strong></p>
    <p><em>It is possible that</em> + futuro se sustituye por un modal de posibilidad (<em>may</em>, <em>might</em> o <em>could</em>) + infinitivo sin <em>to</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B5. A lot of people fear spiders.</strong><br /><strong>(SCARED)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>A lot of people are scared of spiders.</strong></p>
    <p>El verbo <em>fear</em> se sustituye por el adjetivo <em>scared</em>, que va con <em>be</em> y la preposición <em>of</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B6. The environment shouldn’t be polluted.</strong><br /><strong>We…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>We shouldn’t pollute the environment.</strong></p>
    <p>Se pasa de pasiva a activa: el sujeto de la pasiva (<em>the environment</em>) se convierte en objeto, y el modal se mantiene con el infinitivo activo <em>pollute</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 2</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets en-bullets--disc">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B7. All my friends have passed their driving test.</strong><br /><strong>None…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>None of my friends have failed their driving test.</strong></p>
    <p><em>All… have passed</em> equivale a <em>none… have failed</em>: con <em>none</em> la oración ya es negativa, así que el verbo va en afirmativa y se cambia por su contrario (<em>pass</em> → <em>fail</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B8. Is this sweater yours?</strong><br /><strong>(BELONG)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Does this sweater belong to you?</strong></p>
    <p>El pronombre posesivo <em>yours</em> se sustituye por el verbo <em>belong to</em> + pronombre objeto. Al ser un verbo léxico, la pregunta se forma con el auxiliar <em>does</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B9. Bill’s car broke down and he had to take a taxi.</strong><br /><strong>(WHOSE)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Bill, whose car broke / had broken down, had to take a taxi.</strong></p>
    <p>El genitivo <em>Bill’s</em> se sustituye por el relativo posesivo <em>whose</em>, en una relativa explicativa entre comas. Dentro de la relativa vale el pasado simple o el pasado perfecto, porque la avería fue anterior.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B10. ‘I’m sorry I dropped your tablet,’ said Stephen to him.</strong><br /><strong>(APOLOGISED)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Stephen apologised (to him) for dropping / having dropped his tablet.</strong> (o <strong>Stephen apologised that he (had) dropped his tablet.</strong>)</p>
    <p>En estilo indirecto, <em>I’m sorry</em> se resume con <em>apologise (to somebody) for</em> + -ing, o con <em>apologise that</em> + oración. El posesivo <em>your</em> pasa a <em>his</em>, porque la tableta es de la persona a la que habla Stephen.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B11. The last time Taylor Swift sang “Change” at a concert was five years ago.</strong><br /><strong>(FOR)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Taylor Swift hasn’t sung “Change” at a concert for five years.</strong></p>
    <p><em>The last time… was + periodo + ago</em> equivale al present perfect negativo con <em>for</em> + periodo: hace cinco años que no la canta.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B12. I regularly make financial decisions at work.</strong><br /><strong>I am used…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I am used to making financial decisions at work.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Be used to</em> (“estar acostumbrado a”) expresa algo habitual. Aquí <em>to</em> es una preposición, así que va seguido de -ing (<em>making</em>), no de infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
