<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points; 0.5 points each)</strong>. CHOOSE OPTION 1 OR OPTION 2 AND ANSWER <strong><u>ALL</u></strong> THE QUESTIONS FROM THAT OPTION <strong><u>ONLY</u></strong>.</p>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 1</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets en-bullets--disc">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B1. I hate it when you speak to your classmates like that.</strong><br /><strong>If only…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If only you wouldn’t speak to your classmates like that.</strong></p>
    <p>Para quejarse de una costumbre de otra persona que nos molesta se usa <em>if only</em> (o <em>wish</em>) + <em>would(n’t)</em> + infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B2. Carol runs faster than Sheryl.</strong><br /><strong>(AS)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Sheryl can’t run / doesn’t run as (o so) fast as Carol.</strong></p>
    <p>El comparativo de superioridad (<em>faster than</em>) se convierte en comparativo de igualdad negativo (<em>not as/so fast as</em>), invirtiendo los términos.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B3. Rose can’t travel more because she has a very demanding job.</strong><br /><strong>If Rose…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If Rose had a less demanding job, she could travel more.</strong></p>
    <p>Segundo condicional, para una situación presente imaginaria: <em>if</em> + pasado simple, <em>could</em> + infinitivo. Se imagina lo contrario de la realidad: un trabajo menos exigente.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B4. In spite of the rain, they went to the concert.</strong><br /><strong>Although…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Although it was raining, they went to the concert.</strong></p>
    <p><em>In spite of</em> va seguido de un nombre (<em>the rain</em>); <em>although</em> necesita una oración con sujeto y verbo: <em>it was raining</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B5. You mustn’t record TikTok videos at school.</strong><br /><strong>Recording…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Recording TikTok videos at school is forbidden / not allowed / prohibited / banned.</strong></p>
    <p>El gerundio <em>recording</em> funciona como sujeto, y la prohibición de <em>mustn’t</em> se expresa con <em>be</em> + un participio de prohibición (<em>forbidden</em>, <em>not allowed</em>…).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B6. ‘As Mike has forgotten his wallet at home, he can’t pay me back,’ said Paul.</strong><br /><strong>Paul said that Mike…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Paul said that Mike couldn’t pay him back as / because / since he had forgotten his wallet at home.</strong></p>
    <p>En estilo indirecto los tiempos retroceden (<em>can’t</em> → <em>couldn’t</em>, <em>has forgotten</em> → <em>had forgotten</em>) y el pronombre <em>me</em> pasa a <em>him</em>, porque se refiere a Paul.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 2</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets en-bullets--disc">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B7. Producers all over the country are promoting the musical.</strong><br /><strong>The musical…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The musical is being promoted (by producers) all over the country.</strong></p>
    <p>Pasiva del presente continuo: <em>is being</em> + participio. El agente (<em>by producers</em>) puede mantenerse u omitirse.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B8. He trains a lot but he never loses weight.</strong><br /><strong>No matter how…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>No matter how much / hard he trains, he never loses weight.</strong></p>
    <p><em>No matter how</em> + adverbio (“por mucho que”) expresa que el resultado no cambia. <em>A lot</em> se convierte en <em>how much</em> o <em>how hard</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B9. What is the price of that blue cardigan?</strong><br /><strong>(MUCH)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How much does that blue cardigan cost?</strong> (o <strong>How much is that blue cardigan?</strong>)</p>
    <p>Para preguntar por un precio se usa <em>how much</em>, con el verbo <em>cost</em> (y el auxiliar <em>does</em>) o con <em>be</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B10. The treatment known as NewFace reduces wrinkles in two weeks.</strong><br /><strong>NewFace is a…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>NewFace is a treatment which / that reduces wrinkles in two weeks.</strong> (o <strong>NewFace is a treatment to reduce / reducing wrinkles in two weeks.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Se define NewFace con una relativa especificativa (<em>which/that</em>), o con una forma reducida de la relativa: <em>to</em>-infinitivo o -ing.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B11. I don’t have much interest in abstract art.</strong><br /><strong>(INTERESTED)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I am not very / really interested in abstract art.</strong></p>
    <p>El nombre <em>interest</em> pasa al adjetivo <em>interested</em>, que va con <em>be</em> y la preposición <em>in</em>. <em>Not much</em> se convierte en <em>not very/really</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B12. I haven’t had a Japanese meal for ages.</strong><br /><strong>It’s been…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>It’s been ages since I (last) had a Japanese meal.</strong></p>
    <p>El present perfect negativo con <em>for</em> equivale a <em>it’s been</em> + periodo + <em>since</em> + pasado simple, en afirmativa.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
