<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points; 0.5 points each)</strong>. CHOOSE OPTION 1 OR OPTION 2 AND ANSWER <strong><u>ALL</u></strong> THE QUESTIONS FROM THAT OPTION <strong><u>ONLY</u></strong>.</p>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 1</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets en-bullets--disc">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B1. I didn’t tell you because I wasn’t sure.</strong><br /><strong>If…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If I had been sure, I would have told you.</strong></p>
    <p>Tercer condicional, para hablar de una situación pasada que no ocurrió: <em>If</em> + pasado perfecto, <em>would have</em> + participio. Las dos oraciones negativas pasan a afirmativas porque se imagina lo contrario de lo que pasó.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B2. The laundry is too wet to be taken off the clothesline.</strong><br /><strong>The laundry isn’t…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The laundry isn’t dry enough to be taken off the clothesline.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Too</em> + adjetivo equivale a <em>not</em> + adjetivo contrario + <em>enough</em>. <em>Enough</em> va detrás del adjetivo: <em>dry enough</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B3. I would prefer not to go on the field trip.</strong><br /><strong>(RATHER)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I would rather not go on the field trip.</strong> (o <strong>I’d rather not go…</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Would rather</em> equivale a <em>would prefer</em>, pero va seguido de infinitivo sin <em>to</em>. La negación se coloca justo delante del verbo: <em>would rather not go</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B4. They can’t leave the class unless the teacher gives them permission.</strong><br /><strong>(ALLOWED)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They aren’t allowed to leave the class unless the teacher gives them permission.</strong></p>
    <p>El modal <em>can’t</em>, que aquí expresa prohibición, se sustituye por la forma pasiva <em>be (not) allowed to</em> + infinitivo. Como <em>unless</em> equivale a <em>if… not</em>, también es correcto <em>They aren’t allowed to leave the class if the teacher doesn’t give them permission</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B5. I think they should go home.</strong><br /><strong>I think it’s time…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I think it’s time they went home.</strong> (o <strong>I think it’s time for them to go home.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>It’s time</em> + sujeto + pasado simple expresa que algo debería estar ocurriendo ya. El pasado <em>went</em> no se refiere al pasado, sino al presente.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B6. They consider that Manuel is an expert on manga.</strong><br /><strong>Manuel…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Manuel is considered (to be) an expert on manga.</strong></p>
    <p>Pasiva impersonal con sujeto personal: <em>They consider that + sujeto + verbo</em> → <em>Sujeto + is considered + (to be)</em>. Como el sujeto genérico <em>they</em> no aporta información, desaparece.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 2</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets en-bullets--disc">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B7. He wondered why Lisa was so curious about stick insects.</strong><br /><strong>‘Why…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>‘Why is Lisa so curious about stick insects?’ he wondered.</strong></p>
    <p>Se pasa de estilo indirecto a directo: el verbo vuelve al presente (<em>was</em> → <em>is</em>) y recupera el orden de pregunta, con el verbo delante del sujeto. Va entre comillas y con signo de interrogación. Como él se lo pregunta a sí mismo, no a Lisa, se mantiene la tercera persona.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B8. Let’s go to Scotland next summer for a vacation.</strong><br /><strong>How…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How about going to Scotland next summer for a vacation?</strong></p>
    <p>Una sugerencia con <em>Let’s</em> + infinitivo se puede expresar con <em>How about</em> + -ing. Al ser una pregunta, termina con signo de interrogación.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B9. Alice hasn’t smoked for three months.</strong><br /><strong>Alice gave…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Alice gave up smoking three months ago.</strong></p>
    <p>El present perfect negativo con <em>for</em> + periodo se convierte en el pasado simple del <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>give up</em> (“dejar”), seguido de -ing, con <em>ago</em> para indicar cuándo ocurrió.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B10. Urban Flora sometimes delivers gardenia bouquets to Anna.</strong><br /><strong>Anna sometimes gets…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Anna sometimes gets gardenia bouquets delivered by Urban Flora.</strong></p>
    <p>Construcción con <em>get</em> + objeto + participio: Anna recibe los ramos, pero es otro quien realiza la acción. <em>Urban Flora</em> pasa a ser el agente con <em>by</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B11. Alina cannot speak English as well as Dimitri.</strong><br /><strong>Dimitri…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Dimitri speaks English better than Alina.</strong> (o <strong>Dimitri speaks better English than Alina.</strong>)</p>
    <p>El comparativo de igualdad negativo (<em>not as well as</em>) se convierte en comparativo de superioridad invirtiendo los términos. <em>Better</em> es el comparativo irregular de <em>well</em> y de <em>good</em>: puede ir detrás de <em>English</em>, como adverbio (habla inglés mejor), o delante, como adjetivo (habla un inglés mejor). También se acepta mantener el modal: <em>Dimitri can speak English better than Alina</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B12. Masha will be disappointed if she doesn’t get top marks in the exam.</strong><br /><strong>Unless…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Unless Masha gets top marks in the exam, she will be disappointed.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Unless</em> equivale a <em>if… not</em>, así que el verbo de la condición pasa a afirmativa: <em>doesn’t get</em> → <em>gets</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
