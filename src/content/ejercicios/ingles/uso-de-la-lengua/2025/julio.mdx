<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points; 0.5 points each)</strong>. CHOOSE OPTION 1 OR OPTION 2 AND ANSWER <strong><u>ALL</u></strong> THE QUESTIONS FROM THAT OPTION <strong><u>ONLY</u></strong>.</p>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 1</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets en-bullets--disc">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B1. I’ve never been to a more interesting city than London.</strong><br /><strong>The most…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The most interesting city I’ve (ever) been to is London.</strong></p>
    <p>Del comparativo con <em>never</em> (<em>never… a more interesting city than</em>) se pasa al superlativo (<em>the most interesting city</em>). La relativa <em>I’ve (ever) been to</em> va sin pronombre y con la preposición al final. <em>Ever</em> es habitual con el superlativo, pero no es obligatorio.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B2. If he doesn’t wear a hat, he’ll get sunburned.</strong><br /><strong>Unless…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Unless he wears a hat, he will get sunburned.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Unless</em> equivale a <em>if… not</em>, así que el verbo de la condición pasa a afirmativa: <em>doesn’t wear</em> → <em>wears</em>. La oración principal no cambia.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B3. Even though he had a good job, he wasn’t happy.</strong><br /><strong>In spite of…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>In spite of having a good job, he wasn’t happy.</strong> (o <strong>In spite of the fact that he had a good job…</strong> / <strong>In spite of his good job…</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Even though</em> va seguido de una oración con sujeto y verbo; <em>in spite of</em> es una preposición y necesita un nombre, un -ing (<em>having</em>) o la fórmula <em>the fact that</em> + oración.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B4. No foreign languages are required for this job.</strong><br /><strong>(NEEDN’T)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You needn’t speak a/any foreign language for this job.</strong> (también <em>foreign languages</em>)</p>
    <p><em>Needn’t</em> + infinitivo sin <em>to</em> expresa que algo no es necesario, igual que <em>not required</em>. Como el modal necesita un sujeto personal, se usa el genérico <em>you</em> y un verbo adecuado al contexto: <em>speak</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B5. When I dance flamenco, I feel happy.</strong><br /><strong>(MAKES)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Dancing flamenco makes me (feel) happy.</strong></p>
    <p>La acción pasa a ser el sujeto en forma de -ing (<em>dancing flamenco</em>), con la estructura <em>make</em> + persona + adjetivo, o <em>make</em> + persona + infinitivo sin <em>to</em> (<em>feel</em>) + adjetivo. El verbo va en tercera persona: <em>makes</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B6. Vince’s photographs are being shown at the exhibition.</strong><br /><strong>They…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They are showing Vince’s photographs at the exhibition.</strong></p>
    <p>Se pasa de pasiva a activa con un sujeto genérico, <em>they</em>. Se mantiene el presente continuo: <em>are being shown</em> → <em>are showing</em>, y el sujeto de la pasiva pasa a ser el objeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

<p class="en-option"><strong>OPTION 2</strong></p>

<p class="en-instruction">REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES WITHOUT CHANGING THE ORIGINAL MEANING. THE NEW SENTENCE MUST CLEARLY PRESENT A DIFFERENT LINGUISTIC STRUCTURE.</p>

<ul class="en-bullets en-bullets--disc">
  <li>BEGIN AS INDICATED OR USE THE WORD IN BRACKETS.</li>
  <li>THE WORDS PROVIDED MUST NOT BE CHANGED IN ANY WAY.</li>
</ul>

<div class="en-rewrite">
  <p><strong>B7. If I were you, I would eat healthier food.</strong><br /><strong>You had…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You had better eat healthier food.</strong></p>
    <p>Un consejo con <em>If I were you, I would…</em> se puede expresar con <em>had better</em> + infinitivo sin <em>to</em>. Aunque <em>had</em> parece pasado, la estructura se refiere al presente o al futuro.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B8. The groom didn’t buy the engagement ring because it was really expensive.</strong><br /><strong>(SUCH)</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>It was such an expensive engagement ring that the groom didn’t/couldn’t buy it.</strong></p>
    <p>La causa se convierte en una consecutiva con <em>such</em> + <em>a/an</em> + adjetivo + nombre + <em>that</em>. Se usa <em>an</em> porque <em>expensive</em> empieza por sonido vocálico.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B9. I was promoted in 2021.</strong><br /><strong>2021 was the…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>2021 was the year (in which/when/that) I was promoted.</strong></p>
    <p>Se usa una oración de relativo que se refiere a <em>the year</em>. El relativo puede ser <em>when</em>, <em>in which</em> o <em>that</em>, o puede omitirse porque es una relativa especificativa.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B10. My brother asked me if he could borrow &#36;50 until the following Friday.</strong><br /><strong>‘Can…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>‘Can I borrow &#36;50 until next Friday?’ my brother asked me.</strong> (o <strong>‘Can you lend me &#36;50 until next Friday?’ my brother asked me.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Se pasa de estilo indirecto a directo: <em>could</em> vuelve a <em>can</em>, <em>he</em> pasa a <em>I</em>, la pregunta recupera el orden interrogativo y <em>the following Friday</em> vuelve a <em>next Friday</em>. También se puede usar <em>lend</em> (“prestar”) con <em>you</em> como sujeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B11. My parents will celebrate their twenty-fifth wedding anniversary in November.</strong><br /><strong>By the end of the year, …</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>By the end of the year, my parents will have celebrated their twenty-fifth anniversary.</strong> (o <strong>…will have been married for twenty-five years.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Con <em>by</em> + momento futuro se usa el futuro perfecto (<em>will have</em> + participio) para una acción que estará terminada antes de ese momento. Como noviembre es antes del final de año, el aniversario ya se habrá celebrado.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p><strong>B12. I wish you had met my Erasmus mates.</strong><br /><strong>It’s a pity…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>It’s a pity (that) you haven’t met/didn’t meet my Erasmus mates.</strong></p>
    <p><em>I wish</em> + pasado perfecto lamenta algo que no ocurrió. Con <em>It’s a pity (that)</em> se describe la realidad, por eso la oración pasa a negativa, en present perfect o en pasado simple.</p>
  </Solucion>
