<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“I need to have my hair… .”</strong> (do)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“They agreed… at 7 p.m.”</strong> (meet)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “I need to have my hair <strong>done</strong>.”</p>
    <p>Estructura causativa <em>have</em> + objeto + participio: otra persona me arregla el pelo.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “They agreed <strong>to meet</strong> at 7 p.m.”</p>
    <p><em>Agree</em> va seguido de <em>to</em> + infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“Do Laura and Tom get… with each other?”</strong> across / along / down / over</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“We’ve got… time before the plane leaves. Shall we get a coffee?”</strong> a lot / a few / a little / many</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “Do Laura and Tom get <strong>along</strong> with each other?”</p>
    <p><em>Get along with</em> significa “llevarse bien con”.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “We’ve got <strong>a little</strong> time before the plane leaves.”</p>
    <p><em>Time</em> es incontable, así que se descartan <em>a few</em> y <em>many</em>; <em>a lot</em> necesitaría <em>of</em>. <em>A little</em> significa “un poco”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“When will the announcement be made?”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>When will they/you make the announcement?</strong></p>
    <p>Como la pasiva no indica quién hará el anuncio, se usa un sujeto genérico (<em>they</em> o <em>you</em>) y se mantiene el futuro.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“They didn’t give him what he needed.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>He wasn’t given what he needed.</strong> (o <strong>What he needed wasn’t given to him.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Give</em> tiene dos objetos, así que cualquiera puede ser el sujeto de la pasiva negativa en pasado (<em>wasn’t given</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“He persuaded John <u>by giving him a bunch of flowers</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How did he persuade John?</strong></p>
    <p>Se pregunta por el modo con <em>how</em>, con <em>did</em> y el verbo en infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“The tree was very large. We had a nap under it yesterday.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The tree under which we had a nap yesterday was very large.</strong> (o <strong>The tree (which/that) we had a nap under yesterday was very large.</strong>)</p>
    <p>El pronombre <em>it</em> se sustituye por <em>which</em>; la preposición <em>under</em> puede ir delante del relativo o al final.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“Despite his age, my uncle still runs marathons.”</strong> Although...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Although my uncle is (very/quite) old (o not young), he still runs marathons.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Despite</em> va seguido de un nombre (<em>his age</em>); <em>although</em> necesita una oración con sujeto y verbo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“Unless they are paid for it, ...”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Unless they are paid for it, they won’t do the job.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p><em>Unless</em> (“a menos que”) va con presente; la principal puede llevar presente simple, <em>will/can/may…</em> + infinitivo, <em>be going to</em> o un imperativo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“If you read many books, you will gain more knowledge.”</strong> The more…</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The more books you read, the more knowledge you (will) gain.</strong></p>
    <p>Estructura <em>the more…, the more…</em> (“cuanto más…, más…”).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“The others passengers are asking me who suitcase this is.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The <u>other</u> passengers are asking me <u>whose</u> suitcase this is.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: <em>other</em> delante de un nombre es adjetivo y no lleva <em>-s</em>. Segundo error: para preguntar por el dueño se usa <em>whose</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“Tom said: ‘I will do my best at the competition tomorrow’.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Tom said (that) he would do his best at the competition the following/next day.</strong> (o <strong>Tom promised to do his best at the competition the following/next day.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Will</em> retrocede a <em>would</em>, <em>I/my</em> pasan a <em>he/his</em> y <em>tomorrow</em> a <em>the following day</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>wrong</span><span>?</span><span>my</span><span>said</span><span>teacher</span><span>that</span><span>who</span><span>was</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Who said that my teacher was wrong?</strong></p>
    <p>Pregunta de sujeto con <em>who</em>, sin auxiliar, seguida de <em>said that</em> + oración.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
