<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“The baby was sleeping when the telephone… .”</strong> (ring)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“She must… already because the light is off.”</strong> (leave)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “The baby was sleeping when the telephone <strong>rang</strong>.”</p>
    <p>Una acción breve (pasado simple) interrumpe otra que estaba en curso (pasado continuo). <em>Rang</em> es el pasado irregular de <em>ring</em>.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “She must <strong>have left</strong> already because the light is off.”</p>
    <p>Deducción sobre el pasado: <em>must have</em> + participio.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“Has she recovered… the accident yet?”</strong> from / in / up / on</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“The painting is similar… the one I have in my bedroom.”</strong> to / like / in / of</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “Has she recovered <strong>from</strong> the accident yet?”</p>
    <p><em>Recover from</em> significa “recuperarse de”.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “The painting is similar <strong>to</strong> the one I have in my bedroom.”</p>
    <p><em>Similar to</em> significa “parecido a”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“All non-essential shops will be closed.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They will close all non-essential shops.</strong></p>
    <p>Como la pasiva no indica quién cierra las tiendas, se usa el sujeto genérico <em>they</em> y se mantiene el futuro.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“When did they translate this novel into Japanese?”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>When was this novel translated into Japanese?</strong></p>
    <p>Pasiva interrogativa en pasado simple: <em>when</em> + <em>was</em> + sujeto + participio. El agente <em>they</em> se omite.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“<u>Richard’s</u> wife is a famous writer.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Whose wife is a famous writer?</strong></p>
    <p>Para preguntar por el poseedor se usa <em>whose</em> + nombre; como se pregunta por el sujeto, el orden es el de una afirmativa.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“New York is the largest American city. It is not the capital of the US.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>New York, which is not the capital of the US, is the largest American city.</strong> (o <strong>New York, which is the largest American city, is not the capital of the US.</strong>)</p>
    <p>El pronombre <em>it</em> se sustituye por <em>which</em>. Como <em>New York</em> es un nombre propio, la relativa es explicativa y va entre comas.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“That is the most terrifying book I have ever read.”</strong> I have never read...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I have never read such a terrifying book.</strong> (o <strong>I have never read a book (which/that is) so terrifying.</strong>)</p>
    <p>El superlativo con <em>ever</em> se convierte en <em>never</em> + <em>such a</em> + adjetivo + nombre, o + <em>so</em> + adjetivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“If your proposal had been more interesting, ...”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If your proposal had been more interesting, we would have accepted it.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Tercer condicional: sujeto + <em>could(n’t)/would(n’t) have</em> + participio. También vale un condicional mixto con consecuencia presente (<em>…, we would be working on it now</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“When was the last time you heard from her?”</strong> When did…</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>When did you last hear from her?</strong></p>
    <p><em>The last time</em> se sustituye por el adverbio <em>last</em>, y la pregunta en pasado simple lleva <em>did</em> + infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“We ought to not have ordered so many fast food.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>We <u>ought not to</u> have ordered so <u>much</u> fast food.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: la negación de <em>ought to</em> es <em>ought not to</em>. Segundo error: <em>fast food</em> es incontable, así que va con <em>much</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘Can you meet me at the station tomorrow?’, she asked me.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>She asked me to meet her at the station the following/next day.</strong> (o <strong>She asked me if I could meet her at the station the following/next day.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Una petición se puede transmitir con <em>ask</em> + persona + <em>to</em>-infinitivo, o como pregunta indirecta con <em>if</em>. <em>Me</em> pasa a <em>her</em> y <em>tomorrow</em> a <em>the following day</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>a</span><span>used</span><span>blue</span><span>there</span><span>here</span><span>be</span><span>wall</span><span>to</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>There used to be a blue wall here.</strong></p>
    <p><em>There used to be</em> (“antes había”) expresa una situación pasada que ya no existe.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
