<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“Why don’t we go out? I fancy… a drink with you.”</strong> (have)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“When they arrived home, they realised they… the door open.”</strong> (leave)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “Why don’t we go out? I fancy <strong>having</strong> a drink with you.”</p>
    <p><em>Fancy</em> (“apetecer”) va seguido de -ing.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “When they arrived home, they realised they <strong>had left</strong> the door open.”</p>
    <p>Acción anterior a otra del pasado: pasado perfecto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“The final exam consists… ten brief questions.”</strong> on / from / into / of</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“I apologised… being late at work.”</strong> for / with / by / from</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “The final exam consists <strong>of</strong> ten brief questions.”</p>
    <p><em>Consist of</em> significa “constar de”.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “I apologised <strong>for</strong> being late at work.”</p>
    <p><em>Apologise for</em> + -ing significa “disculparse por”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“Life quality is being improved by several interesting discoveries.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Several interesting discoveries are improving life quality.</strong></p>
    <p>El agente pasa a ser el sujeto y se mantiene el presente continuo, en plural: <em>are improving</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“She has just given us the exam.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>We have just been given the exam.</strong> (o <strong>The exam has just been given to us.</strong>)</p>
    <p><em>Give</em> tiene dos objetos, así que cualquiera puede ser el sujeto. Pasiva del present perfect: <em>have/has just been</em> + participio.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“He has been waiting in the queue <u>for thirty minutes</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How long has he been waiting in the queue?</strong></p>
    <p>Se pregunta por la duración con <em>how long</em>, con el auxiliar <em>has</em> delante del sujeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“Jimmy is my favourite cousin. Jimmy’s partner is my classmate.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Jimmy, whose partner is my classmate, is my favourite cousin.</strong></p>
    <p>El genitivo <em>Jimmy’s</em> se sustituye por el relativo posesivo <em>whose</em>, en una relativa explicativa entre comas.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“I regret not going to your birthday party yesterday.”</strong> I wish...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I wish I had gone to your birthday party yesterday.</strong> (o <strong>I wish I could have gone…</strong>)</p>
    <p>Para lamentar algo del pasado se usa <em>wish</em> + pasado perfecto, en afirmativa porque se desea lo contrario de lo que ocurrió.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“If you ever get bored watching youtubers, ...”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If you ever get bored watching youtubers, you could read a good book.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>La principal puede llevar un consejo o sugerencia: <em>you can/may/could/might/should</em> + infinitivo, o <em>why not / why don’t you…?</em></p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“Mike is fixing my car right now.”</strong> I...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I am having/getting my car fixed right now by Mike.</strong></p>
    <p>Estructura causativa <em>have/get</em> + objeto + participio en presente continuo, con el agente introducido por <em>by</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“My uncle didn’t seen nobody breaking into my house last night.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>My uncle didn’t <u>see</u> <u>anybody</u> breaking into my house last night.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: tras <em>didn’t</em> el verbo va en infinitivo. Segundo error: en inglés no se usa doble negación, así que con <em>didn’t</em> se usa <em>anybody</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO DIRECT SPEECH: <strong>“Pam advised me to stop drinking coffee.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Pam said to me / Pam told me: “You should (o ought to) stop drinking coffee.”</strong> (o <strong>“You’d better stop drinking coffee.”</strong>)</p>
    <p>Un consejo en estilo indirecto (<em>advised me to</em>) se expresa en estilo directo con <em>should</em>, <em>ought to</em> o <em>had better</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>driving</span><span>am</span><span>to</span><span>getting</span><span>the</span><span>I</span><span>left</span><span>used</span><span>on</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I am getting used to driving on the left.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Get used to</em> + -ing significa “acostumbrarse a”; aquí, en presente continuo.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
