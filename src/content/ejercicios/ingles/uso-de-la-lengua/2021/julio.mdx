<p class="en-task"><strong>II * USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“My parents are looking forward to… my boyfriend.”</strong> (meet)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“I usually train a lot but I… so much exercise lately.”</strong> (not do)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “My parents are looking forward to <strong>meeting</strong> my boyfriend.”</p>
    <p>En <em>look forward to</em>, <em>to</em> es una preposición, así que va seguido de -ing.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “I usually train a lot but I <strong>haven’t done</strong> so much exercise lately.”</p>
    <p>Con <em>lately</em> (“últimamente”) se usa el present perfect, porque se habla de un periodo que llega hasta el presente.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“I am counting… you to help me get through this.”</strong> from / in / up / on</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“Come on! Drink… ! We need to go!”</strong> at / to / up / out</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “I am counting <strong>on</strong> you to help me get through this.”</p>
    <p><em>Count on somebody</em> significa “contar con alguien”.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “Come on! Drink <strong>up</strong>! We need to go!”</p>
    <p>El <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>drink up</em> significa “terminarse la bebida”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“Long sentences were given to the politicians accused of stealing.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They/The jury/The judge gave the politicians accused of stealing long sentences.</strong> (o <strong>They/The jury/The judge gave long sentences to the politicians accused of stealing.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Como la pasiva no indica quién impuso las condenas, en activa se usa un sujeto genérico (<em>they</em>) o el agente lógico (<em>the jury</em>, <em>the judge</em>). <em>Give</em> tiene dos objetos: la persona puede ir delante sin preposición o detrás con <em>to</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“People may only leave home for specific reasons.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Home may only be left for specific reasons.</strong> (o <strong>Home may be left for specific reasons only.</strong>)</p>
    <p>El objeto (<em>home</em>) pasa a ser el sujeto. Con un modal, la pasiva se forma con modal + <em>be</em> + participio: <em>may be left</em>. El agente genérico <em>people</em> se omite.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“He is being paid <u>2,000€</u> for this project.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How much (money) is he being paid for this project?</strong></p>
    <p>Para preguntar por una cantidad de dinero se usa <em>how much</em>. En presente continuo pasivo, el auxiliar <em>is</em> va delante del sujeto: <em>is he being paid</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“This castle is the most visited tourist attraction in this city. Queen Elizabeth spends her summer holidays in this castle.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>This castle, where/in which Queen Elizabeth spends her summer holidays, is the most visited tourist attraction in this city.</strong> (o <strong>Queen Elizabeth spends her summer holidays in this castle, which is the most visited tourist attraction in this city.</strong>)</p>
    <p>Si el relativo sustituye a <em>in this castle</em>, se usa <em>where</em> o <em>in which</em>; si sustituye a <em>this castle</em> como sujeto, <em>which</em>. Como el castillo ya está identificado, la relativa es explicativa y va entre comas.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“It is not necessary to book a table at that restaurant because it is never full.” You…</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You do not need to book (o do not have to book / needn’t book) a table at that restaurant because it is never full.</strong></p>
    <p><em>It is not necessary</em> se expresa con un sujeto personal (<em>you</em>, genérico) y un verbo de ausencia de obligación: <em>don’t need to</em>, <em>don’t have to</em> o <em>needn’t</em> + infinitivo sin <em>to</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“Had I finished the project on time, …”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Had I finished the project on time, I would have got a better mark.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p><em>Had I finished</em> es la forma invertida, sin <em>if</em>, de <em>If I had finished</em>: un tercer condicional. La oración principal lleva sujeto + <em>could(n’t)/would(n’t) have</em> + participio. Si la consecuencia es presente, también vale <em>could(n’t)/would(n’t)</em> + infinitivo (<em>…, I would be on holiday now</em>). Cualquier final con sentido y con esa estructura es correcto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“Nobody in his class plays chess as well as Tom.” Tom is...</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Tom is the best chess player in his class.</strong></p>
    <p>Que nadie juegue tan bien como Tom significa que él es el mejor: el comparativo de igualdad negativo se convierte en un superlativo. Como empieza por <em>Tom is</em>, el verbo <em>plays</em> pasa al nombre <em>chess player</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THIS SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“She has used never her mother’s car because it has the steering wheel in the right.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>She has <u>never used</u> her mother’s car because it has the steering wheel <u>on</u> the right.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: el adverbio <em>never</em> va entre el auxiliar y el participio (<em>has never used</em>). Segundo error: para indicar el lado se usa <em>on the right/left</em>, no <em>in</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO DIRECT SPEECH: <strong>“John told his mother not to go into his room.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>“Mum, don’t go into my room,” John said.</strong> (o <strong>John told his mother: “Don’t go into my room.”</strong>)</p>
    <p>Una orden negativa en estilo indirecto (<em>told… not to</em> + infinitivo) se convierte en un imperativo negativo (<em>Don’t</em> + infinitivo) entre comillas. El posesivo <em>his</em> pasa a <em>my</em>, porque ahora habla John.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>Friday</span><span>repaired</span><span>she</span><span>next</span><span>have</span><span>car</span><span>will</span><span>her</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>She will have her car repaired next Friday.</strong></p>
    <p>Es la estructura causativa <em>have</em> + objeto + participio, en futuro: ella no arreglará el coche, sino que lo llevará a arreglar. La expresión de tiempo <em>next Friday</em> va al final.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
