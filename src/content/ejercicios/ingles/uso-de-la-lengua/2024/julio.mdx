<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“When hanging out with your friends, avoid … junk food.”</strong> (buy)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“At 9 p.m. tomorrow I … over Cambridge.”</strong> (fly)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “When hanging out with your friends, avoid <strong>buying</strong> junk food.”</p>
    <p>El verbo <em>avoid</em> va siempre seguido de -ing.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “At 9 p.m. tomorrow I <strong>will be flying</strong> over Cambridge.”</p>
    <p>Se usa el futuro continuo (<em>will be</em> + -ing) para una acción que estará en curso en un momento concreto del futuro: mañana a las nueve.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“There is … noise in the classroom.”</strong> too many / too much / few / a lot</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“Lisa came … a gold ring in the sand.”</strong> up / off / round / across</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “There is <strong>too much</strong> noise in the classroom.”</p>
    <p><em>Noise</em> es incontable, así que va con <em>much</em>; <em>too many</em> y <em>few</em> son para contables, y <em>a lot</em> necesitaría <em>of</em> (<em>a lot of noise</em>).</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “Lisa came <strong>across</strong> a gold ring in the sand.”</p>
    <p>El <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>come across</em> significa “encontrar por casualidad”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“Will Ed Sheeran’s new album be released soon?”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Will they release Ed Sheeran’s new album soon?</strong></p>
    <p>Como la pasiva no indica quién hace la acción, en activa se usa el sujeto genérico <em>they</em>. Se mantiene el futuro: <em>will be released</em> → <em>will… release</em>, y el sujeto de la pasiva pasa a ser el objeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“You may see dolphins in this area if the sea is calm.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Dolphins may be seen in this area if the sea is calm.</strong></p>
    <p>El objeto (<em>dolphins</em>) pasa a ser el sujeto. Con un modal, la pasiva se forma con modal + <em>be</em> + participio: <em>may be seen</em>. El sujeto genérico <em>you</em> no aporta información y se omite.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“The museum is <u>just two miles</u> from here.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How far (from here) is the museum (from here)?</strong></p>
    <p>Para preguntar por una distancia se usa <em>how far</em>. Con el verbo <em>be</em> se invierte el orden sujeto-verbo: <em>is the museum</em>. <em>From here</em> puede ir detrás de <em>how far</em> o al final.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“Ann’s favourite dish is chicken Tikka Masala. She buys it at the local shop.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Ann’s favourite dish is chicken Tikka Masala, which she buys at the local shop.</strong> (o <strong>Ann’s favourite dish, which she buys at the local shop, is chicken Tikka Masala.</strong>)</p>
    <p>El pronombre <em>it</em> se sustituye por el relativo <em>which</em> y desaparece. Es una relativa explicativa, porque solo añade información sobre un plato ya identificado, así que va entre comas y no admite <em>that</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“The podcast was so fascinating that I couldn’t stop listening to it.”</strong> It was such…</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>It was such a fascinating podcast that I couldn’t stop listening to it.</strong></p>
    <p><em>So</em> va delante de un adjetivo solo (<em>so fascinating</em>); <em>such</em> va delante de <em>a/an</em> + adjetivo + nombre (<em>such a fascinating podcast</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE: <strong>“My mother got promoted last month, since...”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>My mother got promoted last month, since she had worked very hard all year.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Aquí <em>since</em> significa “ya que, porque” e introduce una causa, así que debe ir seguido de sujeto + verbo conjugado: en presente simple, present perfect, pasado simple o pasado perfecto (<em>since she is very good at her job</em>, <em>since she has a lot of experience</em>…). Cualquier causa con sentido y con esa estructura es correcta.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“I’m not used to speak in public, but I’m getting better in it.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I’m not used to <u>speaking</u> in public, but I’m getting better <u>at</u> it.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: en <em>be used to</em> (“estar acostumbrado a”), <em>to</em> es una preposición, así que va seguido de -ing. Segundo error: <em>good/better</em> se construye con la preposición <em>at</em>, no <em>in</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO DIRECT SPEECH: <strong>“My colleague asked me to postpone our meeting.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>“Could/Can we/you postpone our meeting(, please)?” asked my colleague / my colleague asked (me).</strong> (o <strong>“Postpone our meeting(, please),” asked my colleague / my colleague asked (me).</strong>)</p>
    <p>Una petición en estilo indirecto (<em>asked me to</em> + infinitivo) se convierte en una pregunta de petición con <em>Could/Can…?</em> o en un imperativo, entre comillas. El posesivo <em>our</em> se mantiene porque la reunión es de los dos.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘If you have any problems with the heater, you can call me tonight,’ the landlord told Sally.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The landlord said to / told Sally (that) if she had any problems with the heater, she could call him that night.</strong></p>
    <p>En estilo indirecto los tiempos retroceden (<em>have</em> → <em>had</em>, <em>can</em> → <em>could</em>), cambian los pronombres (<em>you</em> → <em>she</em>, <em>me</em> → <em>him</em>) y la expresión de tiempo (<em>tonight</em> → <em>that night</em>). <em>That</em> se puede omitir.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>is</span><span>deadline</span><span>to</span><span>established</span><span>the</span><span>he</span><span>miss</span><span>likely</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>He is likely to miss the established deadline.</strong></p>
    <p>La estructura <em>be likely to</em> + infinitivo significa “es probable que”. El participio <em>established</em> funciona como adjetivo y va delante del nombre: <em>the established deadline</em> (“el plazo fijado”).</p>
  </Solucion>
