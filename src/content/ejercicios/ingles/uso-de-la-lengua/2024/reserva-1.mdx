<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“Please, don’t make me ... it again.”</strong> (say)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“By this time next year, I … at Harvard University.”</strong> (study)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “Please, don’t make me <strong>say</strong> it again.”</p>
    <p><em>Make</em> + objeto (“obligar a alguien a hacer algo”) va seguido de infinitivo sin <em>to</em>.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “By this time next year, I <strong>will be studying</strong> at Harvard University.”</p>
    <p>Se usa el futuro continuo (<em>will be</em> + -ing) para una acción que estará en curso en un momento concreto del futuro.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“Some thieves broke … my house last night.”</strong> up / into / across / to</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“The more you work out, the … you feel.”</strong> better / well / best / worst</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “Some thieves broke <strong>into</strong> my house last night.”</p>
    <p>El <em>phrasal verb</em> <em>break into</em> significa “entrar a robar”.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “The more you work out, the <strong>better</strong> you feel.”</p>
    <p>La estructura <em>the</em> + comparativo…, <em>the</em> + comparativo (“cuanto más…, más…”) exige un comparativo: <em>better</em>, no <em>best</em> ni <em>well</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“The Prime Minister is said to be preparing a statement.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>People/They say (that) the Prime Minister is preparing a statement.</strong></p>
    <p>La pasiva con sujeto personal (<em>is said to be</em> + -ing) pasa a activa con un sujeto genérico (<em>people</em> o <em>they</em>) y <em>say (that)</em> + oración con el verbo conjugado.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“Somebody must have dropped my mobile phone in the pool.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>My mobile phone must have been dropped in the pool.</strong></p>
    <p>El objeto pasa a ser el sujeto. Con <em>must have</em> + participio, la pasiva se forma con <em>must have been</em> + participio. El agente <em>somebody</em> se omite.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“I’d rather go <u>to the cinema</u> tonight.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Where would you/I rather go (to) tonight?</strong></p>
    <p>Se pregunta por un lugar con <em>where</em>; el auxiliar <em>would</em> va delante del sujeto. La preposición <em>to</em> puede omitirse o quedarse al final.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“I will never forget my daughter’s graduation day. She gave a moving speech that day.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I will never forget my daughter’s graduation day, when (o in which) she gave a moving speech.</strong></p>
    <p><em>That day</em> se refiere a un momento, así que se sustituye por el relativo de tiempo <em>when</em> o por <em>in which</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“I had never read such a long book.”</strong> It was...</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>It was the longest book (that) I had ever read.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Never… such a long</em> se convierte en un superlativo con <em>ever</em>. Se mantiene el pasado perfecto (<em>had ever read</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“If you had called me earlier, ...”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If you had called me earlier, I would have picked you up at the station.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Tercer condicional: con <em>if</em> + pasado perfecto, la oración principal lleva sujeto + <em>would/could/might have</em> + participio. También vale un condicional mixto con consecuencia presente (<em>…, I wouldn’t be so worried now</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“I am looking forward to go to Australia, which is my favorite country of the whole world.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I am looking forward to <u>going</u> to Australia, which is my favorite country <u>in</u> the whole world.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: en <em>look forward to</em>, <em>to</em> es preposición y va seguido de -ing. Segundo error: con un lugar se dice <em>in the whole world</em>, no <em>of</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO DIRECT SPEECH: <strong>“Sarah said she had lost her keys the night before.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Sarah said, “I lost my keys yesterday night / last night.”</strong></p>
    <p>En estilo directo el pasado perfecto vuelve a pasado simple, los pronombres cambian (<em>she</em> → <em>I</em>, <em>her</em> → <em>my</em>) y <em>the night before</em> pasa a <em>last night</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘We won’t be able to attend your party today,’ they told us.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They said to / told us that they wouldn’t be able to attend our party (on) that day.</strong></p>
    <p><em>Won’t</em> retrocede a <em>wouldn’t</em>, <em>your</em> pasa a <em>our</em> y <em>today</em> a <em>that day</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>?</span><span>inviting</span><span>to</span><span>evening</span><span>about</span><span>tomorrow</span><span>her</span><span>dinner</span><span>how</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How about inviting her to dinner tomorrow evening?</strong></p>
    <p>Sugerencia con <em>How about</em> + -ing. Detrás van el objeto (<em>her</em>), <em>to dinner</em> y la expresión de tiempo; el signo de interrogación cierra la frase.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
