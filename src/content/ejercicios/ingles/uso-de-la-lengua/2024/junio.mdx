<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“Please, let me … when you are coming.”</strong> (know)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“When you arrived, we …”</strong> (already / leave)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “Please, let me <strong>know</strong> when you are coming.”</p>
    <p><em>Let</em> + objeto va seguido de infinitivo sin <em>to</em>: <em>let me know</em>.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “When you arrived, we <strong>had already left</strong>.”</p>
    <p>Se usa el pasado perfecto (<em>had</em> + participio) para una acción anterior a otra acción pasada: ya nos habíamos ido cuando llegaste. <em>Already</em> va entre <em>had</em> y el participio.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“Neither Tom … Claire came to the meeting.”</strong> nor / or / either / neither</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“You need to follow the recipe step … step.”</strong> at / by / on / for</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “Neither Tom <strong>nor</strong> Claire came to the meeting.”</p>
    <p><em>Neither</em> siempre va con <em>nor</em> (“ni… ni…”), igual que <em>either</em> va con <em>or</em>.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “You need to follow the recipe step <strong>by</strong> step.”</p>
    <p><em>Step by step</em> (“paso a paso”) es una expresión fija.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“The accident has been caused by a driver using a mobile phone.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>A driver using a mobile phone has caused the accident.</strong></p>
    <p>El agente (<em>by a driver using a mobile phone</em>) pasa a ser el sujeto y el sujeto de la pasiva (<em>the accident</em>) pasa a ser el objeto. Se mantiene el present perfect: <em>has been caused</em> → <em>has caused</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“Is somebody going to order the pizzas in the end?”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Are the pizzas going to be ordered in the end?</strong></p>
    <p>El objeto (<em>the pizzas</em>, plural) pasa a ser el sujeto, por eso el verbo es <em>are</em>. Con <em>be going to</em> la pasiva se forma con <em>going to be</em> + participio. El agente <em>somebody</em> no aporta información y se omite.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“They sold <u>Anna’s</u> car in just one week.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Whose car did they sell in just one week?</strong></p>
    <p>Para preguntar por el poseedor se usa <em>whose</em> + nombre (<em>whose car</em>). Como el verbo está en pasado simple, la pregunta lleva el auxiliar <em>did</em> y el verbo en infinitivo: <em>sold</em> → <em>did… sell</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“Detroit is the city. They lived there when they were young.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Detroit is the city where (o in which) they lived when they were young.</strong></p>
    <p>El adverbio <em>there</em> se refiere a un lugar (<em>the city</em>), así que se sustituye por el relativo <em>where</em> o por <em>in which</em>, y desaparece.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“I’d rather you didn’t stay long at the party.”</strong> You had…</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>You had better not stay long at the party.</strong></p>
    <p><em>I’d rather you didn’t…</em> expresa una preferencia sobre lo que hace otra persona; con <em>had better not</em> + infinitivo sin <em>to</em> se convierte en un consejo. La negación va detrás de <em>better</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“Unless Jake finds a proper solution, …”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Unless Jake finds a proper solution, the problem will get worse.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Es un primer condicional: si la condición va en presente simple (<em>finds</em>), la oración principal lleva sujeto + <em>will</em>, <em>can</em> o <em>may</em> + infinitivo. También vale sujeto + presente simple si es una verdad general (<em>…, he loses his job</em>). Cualquier final con sentido y con esa estructura es correcto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“Despite of the challenges, we managed completing the project on time.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong><u>Despite</u> the challenges, we managed <u>to complete</u> the project on time.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: <em>despite</em> no lleva <em>of</em> (se confunde con <em>in spite of</em>). Segundo error: <em>manage</em> va seguido de <em>to</em> + infinitivo, no de -ing.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO DIRECT SPEECH: <strong>“The policewoman asked to see my driving license.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>“Can/Could/May I see your driving license (, please)?” the policewoman said to me / asked (me).</strong></p>
    <p>Una petición en estilo indirecto (<em>asked to see</em>) se convierte en una pregunta de petición con <em>Can/Could/May I…?</em>. Cambian los pronombres: <em>my</em> (quien cuenta la historia) pasa a <em>your</em>, porque ahora habla la policía.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘I must return the car to my father next weekend,’ Paul said.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Paul said (that) he had to return the car to his father the following weekend / the weekend after.</strong></p>
    <p>En estilo indirecto, <em>must</em> de obligación pasa a <em>had to</em>; los pronombres cambian (<em>I</em> → <em>he</em>, <em>my</em> → <em>his</em>) y la expresión de tiempo también: <em>next weekend</em> → <em>the following weekend</em> o <em>the weekend after</em>. <em>That</em> se puede omitir.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>should</span><span>apple</span><span>for</span><span>be</span><span>the</span><span>everyone</span><span>bakery</span><span>enough</span><span>at</span><span>pie</span><span>there</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>There should be enough apple pie for everyone at the bakery.</strong></p>
    <p>La estructura es <em>there</em> + modal + <em>be</em> (“debería haber”). <em>Enough</em> va delante del nombre (<em>enough apple pie</em>), seguido de <em>for</em> + persona, y al final el lugar: <em>at the bakery</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
