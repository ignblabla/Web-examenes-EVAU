<p class="en-task"><strong>USE OF ENGLISH (3 points;</strong> 0.5 points each<strong>)</strong>. CHOOSE AND ANSWER <strong><u>ONLY 6</u></strong> (SIX) QUESTIONS.</p>

<div class="en-list en-questions">
  <p class="en-hanging">17. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH A CORRECT FORM OF THE VERB IN BRACKETS:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.1. <strong>“We are used to … early.”</strong> (wake up)</p>
  <p class="en-sub">17.2. <strong>“Mary Jane … the trip because it will rain tomorrow.”</strong> (just / put off)</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>17.1.</strong> “We are used to <strong>waking up</strong> early.”</p>
    <p>En <em>be used to</em> (“estar acostumbrado a”), <em>to</em> es una preposición, así que va seguido de -ing.</p>
    <p><strong>17.2.</strong> “Mary Jane <strong>has just put off</strong> the trip because it will rain tomorrow.”</p>
    <p>Para algo que acaba de ocurrir se usa el present perfect con <em>just</em> entre <em>has</em> y el participio. <em>Put off</em> significa “aplazar”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">18. FILL IN THE GAPS WITH THE CORRECT OPTION:</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.1. <strong>“Send me his phone number … I can contact him for the party.”</strong> in order to / so that / so as to / for</p>
  <p class="en-sub">18.2. <strong>“Adults should prevent teenagers … drinking alcohol.”</strong> from / on / toward / off</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>18.1.</strong> “Send me his phone number <strong>so that</strong> I can contact him for the party.”</p>
    <p>Detrás del hueco hay una oración con sujeto y verbo (<em>I can contact</em>), así que se necesita <em>so that</em>; <em>in order to</em> y <em>so as to</em> van con infinitivo.</p>
    <p><strong>18.2.</strong> “Adults should prevent teenagers <strong>from</strong> drinking alcohol.”</p>
    <p><em>Prevent somebody from</em> + -ing significa “impedir que alguien haga algo”.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">19. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE ACTIVE VOICE: <strong>“Was this cathedral built in the 17<sup>th</sup> century?”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Did they build this cathedral in the 17<sup>th</sup> century?</strong></p>
    <p>Como la pasiva no indica quién la construyó, se usa el sujeto genérico <em>they</em>. La pregunta en pasado simple activo se forma con <em>did</em> + infinitivo.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">20. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO THE PASSIVE VOICE: <strong>“Nobody is cleaning the building at the moment.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>The building isn’t being cleaned at the moment.</strong></p>
    <p>Pasiva del presente continuo (<em>is being</em> + participio). Como el sujeto <em>nobody</em> era negativo, la pasiva va en negativa.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">21. GIVE A QUESTION FOR THE UNDERLINED WORDS: <strong>“I have been living in Dublin <u>for three years</u>.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>How long have you/I been living in Dublin?</strong></p>
    <p>Para preguntar por una duración se usa <em>how long</em>, con el auxiliar <em>have</em> delante del sujeto.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">22. JOIN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCES USING A RELATIVE. MAKE CHANGES IF NECESSARY: <strong>“During the prom, James met Sheryl. He immediately fell in love with her.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>During the prom, James met Sheryl, who(m) he immediately fell in love with.</strong> (o <strong>…Sheryl, with whom he immediately fell in love.</strong>)</p>
    <p>El pronombre <em>her</em> se sustituye por <em>who(m)</em>. La preposición <em>with</em> puede quedarse al final o ir delante de <em>whom</em>. Como Sheryl es un nombre propio, la relativa va entre comas.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">23. REWRITE THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE WITHOUT CHANGING ITS MEANING. BEGIN AS INDICATED: <strong>“They repaired Ken’s car yesterday.”</strong> Ken…</p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Ken got/had his car repaired yesterday.</strong></p>
    <p>Estructura causativa <em>get/have</em> + objeto + participio: Ken encargó la reparación a otros.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">24. COMPLETE THE FOLLOWING CONDITIONAL SENTENCE: <strong>“If I had known about the heavy traffic, …”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>If I had known about the heavy traffic, I would have left home earlier.</strong> (por ejemplo)</p>
    <p>Tercer condicional: sujeto + <em>would/could/might have</em> + participio. También vale un condicional mixto con consecuencia presente (<em>…, I wouldn’t be late now</em>).</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">25. THERE ARE TWO MISTAKES IN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE. FIND THE MISTAKES AND REWRITE THE SENTENCE CORRECTLY: <strong>“I don’t know where did he go in holiday last summer.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>I don’t know where <u>he went</u> <u>on</u> holiday last summer.</strong></p>
    <p>Primer error: en una pregunta indirecta no hay auxiliar ni inversión (<em>where he went</em>). Segundo error: “de vacaciones” es <em>on holiday</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">26. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO DIRECT SPEECH: <strong>“The doctor advised me to take more exercise.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>“You should (o ought to) take more exercise,” the doctor said to / told / advised me.</strong></p>
    <p>Un consejo en estilo indirecto (<em>advised me to</em>) se convierte en estilo directo con <em>should</em> u <em>ought to</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">27. TURN THE FOLLOWING SENTENCE INTO REPORTED SPEECH: <strong>“‘We are moving to a new apartment next month,’ they announced.”</strong></p>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>They announced/said (that) they were moving to a new apartment the following month / the month after.</strong></p>
    <p>El presente continuo retrocede a pasado continuo y <em>next month</em> pasa a <em>the following month</em> o <em>the month after</em>.</p>
  </Solucion>

  <p class="en-hanging">28. USE THE WORDS IN THE BOXES TO MAKE A MEANINGFUL SENTENCE. USE ALL AND ONLY THE WORDS IN THE BOXES WITHOUT CHANGING THEIR FORM:</p>
  <div class="en-words">
    <span>?</span><span>car</span><span>waiting</span><span>in</span><span>you</span><span>me</span><span>mind</span><span>the</span><span>for</span><span>would</span>
  </div>
  <Solucion>
    <p><strong>Would you mind waiting for me in the car?</strong></p>
    <p>Petición educada con <em>Would you mind</em> + -ing (“¿te importaría…?”), seguida de <em>for me</em> y del lugar.</p>
  </Solucion>
</div>
