<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: CYCLING, THE BEST MODE OF TRANSPORT</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">Urban population in developing nations is projected to continue to grow, adding 2.5 billion people to the world’s cities by 2050. By</li>
  <li>then, more than half of the world’s population will live in cities.</li>
  <li class="is-start">A key step to managing rapid urbanization, reducing poverty and addressing equality amongst urban residents is to meet their needs</li>
  <li>for access to services and opportunities. Walking and cycling improve urban quality and facilitate social cohesion. They are cheap,</li>
  <li>flexible, personal modes without which most people in low- and middle-income countries would be unable to participate in the economy</li>
  <li>and community, or access education, healthcare and other urban services. Today, more than 600 cities worldwide have their own bike-</li>
  <li>share systems, and more programmes are starting every year.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In richer countries, cycling has become popular for a variety of reasons. For instance, it helps to reduce the risk of diabetes, some</li>
  <li>forms of cancer, cardiovascular diseases, depression, and it prevents obesity. The health benefits of cycling daily rather than taking a</li>
  <li>car for short trips outweigh the risks of inhalation of air pollutants. Besides, regular cycling boosts physical fitness.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Riding a bike evokes pleasant feelings and is an easy exercise. People who walk or cycle to work tend to be more satisfied, less</li>
  <li>stressed, more relaxed, and they experience greater freedom compared to people who drive their car to work. Bicycle use, therefore,</li>
  <li>not only improves physical health, but also has a positive impact on mental health and subjective well-being.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In short, compared to travelling by car or bus, cycling saves money, it is a cheap means of transport, and scores well in terms of</li>
  <li>social impact of urban mobility.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>1. According to the text, the world’s population will…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) increase after 2050 only.</span>
  <span>(b) reach a total of 2.5 billion people.</span>
  <span>(c) settle only in rural communities.</span>
  <span>(d) increase mainly in cities.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(d) increase mainly in cities.</strong></p>
  <p>La población urbana seguirá creciendo y para 2050 más de la mitad de la población mundial vivirá en ciudades (líneas 1-2): <em>“Urban population in developing nations is projected to continue to grow, adding 2.5 billion people to the world’s cities by 2050. By then, more than half of the world’s population will live in cities.”</em></p>
  <p>El crecimiento se producirá de aquí a 2050, no solo después (a); 2.500 millones son las personas que se sumarán a las ciudades, no la población total (b); y la población se concentrará en las ciudades, no en el campo (c).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>2. According to the text, cycling…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) improves the quality of life in cities.</span>
  <span>(b) makes life in cities worse.</span>
  <span>(c) does not build social relationships.</span>
  <span>(d) is better than walking in terms of equality.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(a) improves the quality of life in cities.</strong></p>
  <p>Caminar e ir en bicicleta mejoran la calidad de vida urbana (línea 4): <em>“Walking and cycling improve urban quality and facilitate social cohesion.”</em></p>
  <p>Es justo lo contrario: mejora la vida en las ciudades (b) y favorece la cohesión social (c); y el texto habla de caminar y de la bicicleta a la vez, sin compararlos (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: MO, THE CAT</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">A cat has surprisingly been reunited with his family eight years after he went missing. Mo —the cat— was staying in a cattery on the</li>
  <li>outskirts of Swansea eight years ago, while his humans went on holiday. On their return, one of the family’s cats, Tibby, was waiting</li>
  <li>for them, but they were devastated to find Mo had gone missing. “We were utterly heartbroken when we found out that the cattery had</li>
  <li>lost Mo,” says Izzy Harris, who was a teenager when Mo, then three, went missing.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Izzy and her parents searched for their pet for months, printing flyers and putting adverts in the newspaper. However, they also knew</li>
  <li>there was a small chance they’d see him again. “There was something so horrible about imagining him alone,” continues Izzy, “the</li>
  <li>cattery was near some busy main roads as well, so we couldn’t stop picturing the worst, that he’d been hit by a car.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">After months of searching, the family accepted he was gone, and only hoped that he’d found someone else to live with —and they</li>
  <li>were right! As a matter of fact, Mo had moved in with an elderly lady.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Last October, the Harris family received an unexpected call from a veterinary centre. “They said they’d found a cat matching Mo’s</li>
  <li>microchip and that he’d been living near where he got lost with an old lady who had passed away.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">Izzy and Mo met finally again thanks to the power of microchipping. The black cat has a lot of catching up to do, as the family</li>
  <li>currently has three other cats. The girl says they feel incredibly relieved, but also hopeful. If this can happen, anything can.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>9. The Harris family was greatly upset because...</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) Mo had run away from his family home.</span>
  <span>(b) their holidays were over.</span>
  <span>(c) they didn’t know where Mo was.</span>
  <span>(d) both cats were missing.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) they didn’t know where Mo was.</strong></p>
  <p>La familia se quedó destrozada al descubrir que Mo había desaparecido (línea 3): <em>“they were devastated to find Mo had gone missing.”</em></p>
  <p>Mo se perdió en la residencia felina, no se escapó de casa (a); el disgusto no fue por acabar las vacaciones (b); y la otra gata, Tibby, sí los estaba esperando (d).</p>
</Solucion>
