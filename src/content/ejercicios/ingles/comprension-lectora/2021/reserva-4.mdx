<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: SHOULD DOGS BE ALLOWED IN CAFÉS?</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">Almost half of all UK adults own a pet, and 24% have a dog, with the pet dog population now estimated to be around 9.4 million.</li>
  <li>Mostly, those pet dogs are not welcome inside shops, restaurants and other places with their owners. Naturally, there are exceptions for</li>
  <li>assistance dogs, but the rest find themselves left at home or outside, on the pavement, while the owner pops into a café for a cup of tea.</li>
  <li class="is-start">People think dogs are not allowed into shops and cafés for hygiene reasons, but there is nothing in law to prevent a dog from being</li>
  <li>in a café —only from food-preparation areas. If adequate steps are taken to keep food safe, it is up to a café owner whether or not to</li>
  <li>admit pets.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The “Be Dog Friendly” campaign was designed to encourage businesses and public places around the UK to allow dogs. Not all</li>
  <li>dogs would be desirable, however. Badly behaved ones could ruin a pleasant outing —a café in Singapore got into trouble when two</li>
  <li>canines fought each other in front of customers. For this reason, the campaign promotes “petiquette” guidelines to make sure your dog</li>
  <li>will be welcome wherever it goes. Ensuring the dog is clean, well-behaved and under the owner’s control at all times serves everyone’s</li>
  <li>welfare.</li>
  <li class="is-start">For some people, though, the presence of a dog is extremely unwelcome, perhaps because of pet allergies or fear of dogs.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Dog-friendly cafés may be increasing in “pup-ularity”, but they should be well signposted so that anyone who prefers to dine dog-</li>
  <li>free can continue to do so with ease.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>1. “Be Dog Friendly” is a campaign designed to…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) encourage people to go into cafés with a pet.</span>
  <span>(b) train dogs in the UK.</span>
  <span>(c) permit dogs in more places.</span>
  <span>(d) prevent a dog from being in a café.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) permit dogs in more places.</strong></p>
  <p>La campaña anima a negocios y lugares públicos a admitir perros (línea 7): <em>“designed to encourage businesses and public places around the UK to allow dogs.”</em></p>
  <p>Se dirige a los negocios, no a los dueños (a); da pautas de comportamiento, pero no adiestra perros (b); y busca lo contrario de impedir su entrada (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>2. Some people do not like being near dogs because...</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) they don’t have a dog of their own.</span>
  <span>(b) they are afraid of dogs.</span>
  <span>(c) those people do not like the dogs’ owners.</span>
  <span>(d) dogs keep barking all the time.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) they are afraid of dogs.</strong></p>
  <p>A algunas personas les molestan los perros por alergia o por miedo (línea 12): <em>“perhaps because of pet allergies or fear of dogs.”</em></p>
  <p>El texto no menciona ninguno de los otros motivos (a, c y d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: THE AGED WOMAN</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">Long ago, a poor farmer lived with his aged mother in the land of a tyrannical leader who disliked weak and sick people. One day,</li>
  <li>the ruler decided that old people had to die. Though the farmer loved his mother deeply, he couldn’t disobey an order from the governor</li>
  <li>and so, with a heavy heart, he led his mother to the desolate mountain summit known as Obatsuyama (meaning “abandoning of the</li>
  <li>aged”).</li>
  <li class="is-start">Along the journey, the farmer had trouble navigating the difficult paths of the mountain and his mother grew anxious at the thought</li>
  <li>of her son getting lost and dying on his return. Going unnoticed by her son, she decided to make a way for him to follow by breaking</li>
  <li>twigs off the trees and throwing them along the path. At last, she revealed what she had done and the farmer, moved by her actions,</li>
  <li>proclaimed that he would not leave her there and both would go back to die together.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The farmer hid his mother at home and for a time it seemed that all was peaceful. However, again the governor ordered something</li>
  <li>unreasonable: the people had to make a rope of ashes. When the woman heard the news, and with the wisdom cultivated throughout</li>
  <li>her years, she asked her son to make a rope of straw and burn it on a large flat stone.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The governor heard what the farmer had done and wanted to know where he had acquired such wisdom. It was then that the poor</li>
  <li>man revealed the whole story. The governor listened and finally recognized that the land needed more than the strength and vitality of</li>
  <li>young people and abolished his cruel laws.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>9. The governor of the land…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) had always cared for the elderly.</span>
  <span>(b) saw how the aged woman was taken to Obatsuyama.</span>
  <span>(c) sentenced the farmer to death.</span>
  <span>(d) was at first cruel to old people.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(d) was at first cruel to old people.</strong></p>
  <p>Al principio ordenó que los ancianos murieran, aunque al final abolió sus leyes crueles (línea 2): <em>“the ruler decided that old people had to die.”</em></p>
  <p>Despreciaba a los débiles y a los enfermos (a); no presenció el viaje a la montaña (b); y no condenó al granjero (c).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>10. The story suggests that...</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) we are essential as long as we are young.</span>
  <span>(b) old people are also needed in society.</span>
  <span>(c) the aged woman hated the governor.</span>
  <span>(d) the farmer was very intelligent.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) old people are also needed in society.</strong></p>
  <p>El gobernador reconoce que el país necesita algo más que la fuerza de los jóvenes (líneas 13-14): <em>“the land needed more than the strength and vitality of young people”</em>.</p>
  <p>La historia demuestra lo contrario (a); el texto no habla de odio (c); y la sabiduría era de la madre, no del granjero (d).</p>
</Solucion>
