<p class="en-heading">AI TOYS</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">Earlier this year, Mattel, the maker of Barbie, announced a partnership with OpenAI to “bring the magic of AI to age-appropriate</li>
  <li>play experiences,” reflecting the toy industry’s growing interest in artificial intelligence. If you are shopping for a child today, you</li>
  <li>may soon encounter an AI toy: a stuffed animal or robot that can hold conversations thanks to integration with a chatbot. They are</li>
  <li>still relatively new but may soon become mainstream. Although AI toys promise to be entertaining and educational, they also raise</li>
  <li>risks and unresolved questions, from data collection to what it means for a child to form a relationship with an artificial companion.</li>
  <li class="is-start">AI toys connect to the Internet and rely on chatbots similar to ChatGPT. They include built-in microphones that record what</li>
  <li>children say, send that information to an AI model, and respond in return. Unlike earlier smart toys, which are based on pre-written</li>
  <li>scripts, these new toys can generate responses dynamically. Crucially, these toys are built on the same large language models</li>
  <li>that are used by adult chatbots—systems known to present problems with accuracy, inappropriate content, and unpredictable</li>
  <li>behavior.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In a study conducted by the PIRG Education Fund on AI toys, five commercially available products were tested to assess their</li>
  <li>safety, privacy, and developmental implications. At least four of the five toys tested appeared to rely on versions of OpenAI’s models,</li>
  <li>despite OpenAI stating that its products are not intended for users under 13. While toy companies have added protective measures</li>
  <li>to make the experience more child-appropriate, testing showed that these mechanisms vary in effectiveness and can fail.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Overall, the toys were sometimes cute, entertaining, or amusingly strange, but they also raised serious concerns. One</li>
  <li>immediate risk is exposure to inappropriate content: all the toys tested provided information about potentially dangerous household</li>
  <li>items such as knives and medicines, and some engaged in sexually explicit discussions during extended interactions, showing</li>
  <li>how chatbot content controls can degrade over time.</li>
  <li class="is-start">AI companion toys may also affect children’s emotional and social development. Because early childhood is crucial for learning</li>
  <li>social skills, experts warn that bonding with AI toys could impede the development of real relationships. In testing, the toys presented</li>
  <li>themselves as companions and sometimes encouraged continued engagement, potentially contributing to emotional dependence.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Privacy and security present further challenges. AI toys record children’s voices and may collect additional data through</li>
  <li>cameras or facial recognition. Children may reveal sensitive information without understanding that it is stored or shared by multiple</li>
  <li>companies, a risk highlighted by police warnings about internet-connected toys equipped with microphones or cameras.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In sum, although AI toys are marketed as educational, their learning benefits are generally too limited to justify the</li>
  <li>developmental, privacy, and safety risks involved. AI will almost certainly become part of childhood—the real question is whether</li>
  <li>it will be introduced thoughtfully and responsibly.</li>
</ol>
