<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: WAR LETTERS</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">Sharon Taylor has no firsthand memories of her father. The World War II fighter pilot was shot down over Germany in April</li>
  <li>1945, when Taylor was three weeks old. The war ended less than a month later, and the letters that the 22-year-old pilot had</li>
  <li>exchanged with his wife Mary since they were high school sweethearts stopped coming. Taylor’s father was eventually listed as</li>
  <li>killed in action, although his body was never recovered.</li>
  <li class="is-start">One day, when Taylor was seven years old, she was sipping a hot chocolate while her paternal grandmother shared stories</li>
  <li>about their fallen hero, crying as she remembered her son. “Nana, I’m going to find him and bring him home.” It was a promise</li>
  <li>Taylor, now a 77-year-old author and retired professor, would keep. In 2006 she concluded a multi-decade mission to recover her</li>
  <li>father’s remains and bring them home. She did it guided by clues that she had put together from her parents’ wartime letters —</li>
  <li>along with help from historians, eyewitnesses, and an excavation team.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Now, through a sound and light show at the National World War II Museum in New Orleans, she’s bringing to new generations</li>
  <li>her parents’ experience and a larger story of war casualties and their families. Taylor shares her story widely, raising awareness</li>
  <li>of soldiers who never returned from war and emphasizing the importance of recovery efforts. An estimated 81,000 American</li>
  <li>soldiers’ bodies remain missing, and she wants people to know there are ongoing efforts to locate them.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Taylor will never know exactly what her father’s final moments were like. But as she previews the <em>Expressions of America</em></li>
  <li>exhibition in New Orleans, she somehow feels that he has come home at last.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>1. Taylor’s parents…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) started writing each other during the war.</span>
  <span>(b) were in love when they were teenagers.</span>
  <span>(c) stopped writing each other during the war.</span>
  <span>(d) first met when they were in their twenties.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) were in love when they were teenagers.</strong></p>
  <p>Eran novios desde el instituto (líneas 2-3): <em>“the letters that the 22-year-old pilot had exchanged with his wife Mary since they were high school sweethearts”</em>.</p>
  <p>Se escribían desde el instituto, no desde la guerra (a); las cartas dejaron de llegar cuando él murió, no porque dejaran de escribirse (c); y se conocían desde adolescentes, no desde los veinte años (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>2. Taylor’s search for her father…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) was kept secret from her grandmother.</span>
  <span>(b) led to the discovery of a number of war letters.</span>
  <span>(c) was carried out by other pilots.</span>
  <span>(d) is something she believes is worth being made public.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(d) is something she believes is worth being made public.</strong></p>
  <p>Taylor difunde su historia para concienciar sobre los soldados desaparecidos (líneas 11-12): <em>“Taylor shares her story widely, raising awareness of soldiers who never returned from war”</em>, y la cuenta en un espectáculo de un museo.</p>
  <p>Se lo prometió a su abuela, así que no se lo ocultó (a); las cartas ya existían y le sirvieron de pistas, no las descubrió con la búsqueda (b); y la ayudaron historiadores, testigos y un equipo de excavación, no otros pilotos (c).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: IS WORLD HERITAGE IN DANGER?</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">In 2016 the city government of Vienna announced that a public-private partnership would build a new ice-skating rink just</li>
  <li>outside the city’s century-old concert hall.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Anyone visiting the place where Beethoven and Mozart met will notice two things immediately. First, the core of Vienna is an</li>
  <li>architectural fantasy of baroque palaces, immaculate courtyards, and a neo-Gothic city hall. Second, Austrians love winter sports,</li>
  <li>and ice-skating is as typically Viennese as symphonies and sausages. Therefore, the idea of a permanent ice rink would not have</li>
  <li>been expected to cause controversy. However, the UNESCO World Heritage Committee decreed that the rink would undermine</li>
  <li>the “outstanding universal value” of central Vienna.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Since 2001, Vienna’s city center has been a World Heritage site, that is, one of the organization’s 1,154 unique landmarks</li>
  <li>around the globe. Since announcing its objection to the rink, the World Heritage Committee has kept Vienna on its “in danger” list,</li>
  <li>joining fifty other sites, from the ancient villages of Syria to Everglades National Park in Florida. If the city fails to address the</li>
  <li>committee’s concerns satisfactorily, it risks being permanently “de-listed” as a UNESCO landmark.</li>
  <li class="is-start">UNESCO has recently had to confront a newer enemy: climate change. Here the organisation’s resources to tackle the problem</li>
  <li>are limited too. Last year, UNESCO was forced to threaten Australia with placing its legendary Great Barrier Reef on the “in danger”</li>
  <li>list if its government did not work hard enough to reduce its greenhouse gas emissions.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Even though the World Heritage program may be imperfect and at times powerless, it remains relevant because of its main</li>
  <li>philosophy: the world’s diverse treasures require protection, since they cannot protect themselves.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>9. According to the text, the Viennese…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) are against the city council’s plans.</span>
  <span>(b) are very fond of ice-skating.</span>
  <span>(c) don’t care about UNESCO’s warning.</span>
  <span>(d) are worried about climate change.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) are very fond of ice-skating.</strong></p>
  <p>A los austriacos les encantan los deportes de invierno y el patinaje es una tradición de Viena (líneas 4-5): <em>“Austrians love winter sports, and ice-skating is as typically Viennese as symphonies and sausages.”</em></p>
  <p>No se esperaba que el proyecto causara polémica, así que los vieneses no estaban en contra (a); el texto no dice cómo reaccionaron ante el aviso de la UNESCO (c); y el cambio climático aparece en relación con Australia, no con los vieneses (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>10. According to the text, …</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) both Vienna’s and Australia’s sites are environmentally threatened.</span>
  <span>(b) Vienna’s council has just given up on the rink project.</span>
  <span>(c) Vienna’s status as a World Heritage site is at risk.</span>
  <span>(d) fifty sites have already lost their World Heritage status.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) Vienna’s status as a World Heritage site is at risk.</strong></p>
  <p>Viena está en la lista de patrimonio “en peligro” y puede perder la distinción (líneas 10-11): <em>“If the city fails to address the committee’s concerns satisfactorily, it risks being permanently ‘de-listed’ as a UNESCO landmark.”</em></p>
  <p>La amenaza para Viena es la pista de hielo, no un problema medioambiental (a); el texto no dice que se haya abandonado el proyecto (b); y los otros cincuenta lugares están en la lista “en peligro”, pero no han perdido aún su estatus (d).</p>
</Solucion>
