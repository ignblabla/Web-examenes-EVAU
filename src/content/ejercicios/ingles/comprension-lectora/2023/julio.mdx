<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: STAYING ACTIVE IN LATER LIFE</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">A healthy lifestyle matters at all ages but probably more as we get older. However, many old people feel that they have reached</li>
  <li>an age when exercise is too tiring, and that it is too late to worry about dietary problems. Nevertheless, those who are active into</li>
  <li>older age see a reduced risk of developing heart disease, obesity or diabetes. Doctors recommend that old people should quit</li>
  <li>smoking, eat a healthy diet and practise some light activity, such as daily walking, to improve their quality of life.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Paul, an 82-year-old Englishman who has lived in southern Spain for 40 years, believes it is imperative to fight obesity with a</li>
  <li>healthy diet and regular exercise: “The only time I go to a fast-food restaurant is when I am in England visiting my grandchildren.”</li>
  <li>Additionally, he has never smoked and rarely eats meat, adhering to a diet of fish, lentils and salad instead.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Paul has two young grandchildren who do not fully understand the importance of healthy habits. Concerned about their</li>
  <li>wellbeing, he says, “it’s complicated to tell my grandchildren not to eat junk food or explain to them the importance of exercise, but</li>
  <li>fortunately my daughter exercises a lot, so maybe they will follow our example.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">Himself a very active man, Paul is no stranger to physical exercise: “I began boxing in my youth, and then I was the proud</li>
  <li>owner of a rather successful fitness centre in Birkenhead for over 30 years,” he states. “Now I keep myself fit working out at home,</li>
  <li>in my little basement gym. I also walk twelve kilometres along the beach every other day, and use the exercise apparatus installed</li>
  <li>close to the beach.”</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>1. According to the text, many older people…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) are eager to go on a diet.</span>
  <span>(b) cannot exercise due to heart disease, obesity and diabetes.</span>
  <span>(c) find physical activity exhausting.</span>
  <span>(d) develop serious illnesses after doing regular exercise.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) find physical activity exhausting.</strong></p>
  <p>Muchas personas mayores sienten que el ejercicio les resulta demasiado cansado (líneas 1-2): <em>“many old people feel that they have reached an age when exercise is too tiring”</em>.</p>
  <p>Creen que ya es tarde para preocuparse por la dieta (<em>“too late to worry about dietary problems”</em>), así que no están deseando hacerla (a); el texto dice que el ejercicio reduce el riesgo de esas enfermedades, no que impidan hacerlo (b); y es justo lo contrario: quienes se mantienen activos tienen menos riesgo de enfermar (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>2. According to the text, Paul…</strong></p>

  <span>(c) has inspired his whole family to be very sporty.</span>
  <span>(d) had to take up sport following the doctor’s orders.</span>

<Solucion>

  <p>Paul confía en que sus nietos acaben siguiendo el ejemplo de su madre y el suyo (línea 10): <em>“fortunately my daughter exercises a lot, so maybe they will follow our example.”</em></p>
  <p>Lleva 40 años en España y tiene 82, así que no ha vivido allí la mayor parte de su vida (a); sus nietos todavía no entienden la importancia de los hábitos saludables (c); y hace deporte desde joven por iniciativa propia, no por orden médica (<em>“I began boxing in my youth”</em>) (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: THE HISTORY OF JEANS</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">The word <em>jeans</em> started out in the 1800s, in reference to a cotton cloth used for trousers. Blue jeans, now also called <em>denim</em>,</li>
  <li>were originally made from this fabric and manufactured in the French town of Nîmes. By the 20<sup>th</sup> century, <em>jeans</em> was the term for</li>
  <li>denim informal trousers.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The most recognizable, classic jeans were patented in 1873 by Jacob Davis, a tailor, and Levi Strauss, owner of a fabric house</li>
  <li>in San Francisco. Strauss and Davis initially made jeans in two types of fabric, brown cotton and blue denim, but the creation of</li>
  <li>the denim 501 style in 1890 helped the latter fabric take off.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Hollywood helped romanticize blue jeans in the 1920s and 1930s by putting the trousers on handsome cowboy types played</li>
  <li>by actors John Wayne and Gary Cooper. This glamorous new image appealed to consumers who sought casual leisurewear for</li>
  <li>the weekends and holidays. Publicity photos of famous actresses like Ginger Rogers and Carole Lombard wearing jeans helped</li>
  <li>convince women that the style was for them too. In the 1930s, <em>Vogue</em> gave their approval, calling jeans “Western chic”.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Yet, it wasn’t until the 1950s that jeans came to be associated with rebellious youth. Hippies wore jeans in the 1960s and early</li>
  <li>1970s as a way to show support for the working class, while feminists and women’s rights activists chose blue jeans as a way to</li>
  <li>demonstrate gender equality.</li>
  <li class="is-start">“I have often said that I wish I had invented blue jeans,” Yves Saint Laurent told <em>New York Magazine</em> in November 1983. “They</li>
  <li>have expression, modesty, sex appeal, simplicity… all that I hope for in my clothes.”</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>9. According to the text, the word <em>jeans</em> originally referred to…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) informal trousers.</span>
  <span>(b) cowboys and the Far West.</span>
  <span>(c) a kind of fabric.</span>
  <span>(d) the French town of Nîmes.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) a kind of fabric.</strong></p>
  <p>Al principio la palabra designaba un tejido de algodón (línea 1): <em>“The word jeans started out in the 1800s, in reference to a cotton cloth used for trousers.”</em></p>
  <p>Solo en el siglo XX pasó a designar los pantalones (<em>“By the 20th century, jeans was the term for denim informal trousers”</em>) (a); la asociación con los vaqueros del cine llegó en los años 20 y 30 (b); y Nîmes es el lugar donde se fabricaba el tejido, no el significado de la palabra (d).</p>
</Solucion>

  <span>(c) hippies used to wear them.</span>

<Solucion>

  <p>En los años 20 y 30, Hollywood los puso de moda con actores como John Wayne y Gary Cooper (líneas 7-8): <em>“Hollywood helped romanticize blue jeans in the 1920s and 1930s by putting the trousers on handsome cowboy types played by actors John Wayne and Gary Cooper.”</em> Las fotos de actrices como Ginger Rogers hicieron lo mismo entre las mujeres.</p>
  <p>El texto no habla de publicidad de Levi Strauss (a); los <em>hippies</em> los llevaron en los años 60 y 70, en la segunda mitad del siglo (c); y la entrevista de <em>New York Magazine</em> es de 1983 (d).</p>
</Solucion>
