<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: SOMETHING BORROWED, SOMETHING BLUE…</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">Last August, a 911 operator came to the rescue of a desperate bride, but not in the way you would expect. A bride named</li>
  <li>Amanda had her wedding dress stolen on the morning of her nuptials! She had been packing her car when, upon returning from a</li>
  <li>quick trip to the house, she discovered the dress had gone missing. Panicking, she called 911 to report the theft.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Candice, the 911 dispatcher answering the call, couldn’t help but sympathize with Amanda. She said that all she kept thinking</li>
  <li>was “I have a wedding gown. Maybe she could wear that. Here we go, let’s see if she wants it.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">On an impulse, Candice asked her supervisor if she could offer the bride her dress. Of course, she had no idea if the dress</li>
  <li>would fit, much less if Amanda would even want to give it a try. Luckily, her supervisor agreed and let her do it. Though grateful</li>
  <li>for the offer, the traumatized bride asked to see a picture of the gown first. Candice completely understood. She texted the police</li>
  <li>officer on the scene a picture of the gown and, thankfully, Amanda loved it.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The operator then asked her husband to pick up the dress, which was in her parents’ attic. After overcoming a series of</li>
  <li>obstacles, including discovering that he had taken the wrong keys, Candice’s husband successfully delivered the dress to the bride</li>
  <li>in time, and the wedding went off without any further incidents.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Some days later, newlywed Amanda surprised Candice at her office to return her dress. She wanted to say a personal and</li>
  <li>heartfelt thank you to the woman that she believes saved her wedding.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>1. According to the text, the person who took Candice’s gown to the bride was…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) Candice herself.</span>
  <span>(b) the police officer on the scene.</span>
  <span>(c) Candice’s husband.</span>
  <span>(d) Candice’s supervisor.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) Candice’s husband.</strong></p>
  <p>El marido de Candice llevó el vestido a tiempo (líneas 11-12): <em>“Candice’s husband successfully delivered the dress to the bride in time”</em>.</p>
  <p>Candice pidió a su marido que fuera a buscarlo (a); a la policía solo le envió una foto (b); y su supervisora únicamente le dio permiso (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>2. According to the text, …</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) desperate Amanda didn’t accept the offer immediately.</span>
  <span>(b) Candice attended the wedding ceremony.</span>
  <span>(c) Candice’s husband forgot to take the keys.</span>
  <span>(d) the wedding gown needed some minor alterations.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(a) desperate Amanda didn’t accept the offer immediately.</strong></p>
  <p>Antes de aceptar, Amanda pidió ver una foto del vestido (líneas 7-8): <em>“Though grateful for the offer, the traumatized bride asked to see a picture of the gown first.”</em></p>
  <p>Se conocieron días después, cuando Amanda fue a devolver el vestido (b); el marido se llevó las llaves equivocadas, no se olvidó de ellas (c); y el texto no habla de arreglos en el vestido (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: SANAS CAN CHANGE YOUR ACCENT</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">English is the world’s most widely used language. An estimated 1.5 billion people speak it, and most of them aren’t native</li>
  <li>speakers. In the United States alone, millions of people speak English as a second language. A new company called Sanas is</li>
  <li>testing out artificial intelligence-powered software that aims to eliminate miscommunication by changing people’s accents in real</li>
  <li>time. A call center worker in the Philippines, for example, could speak normally into the microphone and end up sounding more</li>
  <li>like someone from Kansas to a customer on the other end. The American accent sounds somewhat artificial, Siri-like, but they are</li>
  <li>working on improving the technology. The idea is that, rather than learning to pronounce words differently, you could have</li>
  <li>technology do it for you. And understanding would be nearly instantaneous.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The three founders of Sanas met at Stanford University, but they’re all from different countries originally. All three say they’ve</li>
  <li>experienced over the years how accents can get in the way. “We all come from international backgrounds. We’ve seen firsthand</li>
  <li>how people treat you differently just because of the way you speak,” Maxim Serebryakov, one of the founders, says. He also</li>
  <li>remarks that people’s accents and identities can be closely linked, and he stresses that the company isn’t trying to erase accents</li>
  <li>or imply that one way of speaking is better than another.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Eventually, they hope the app they’re developing will be used by a variety of industries and individuals. It could help doctors</li>
  <li>better understand patients. Someday, they say, it could also assist language learners and improve dubbing in movies. It could</li>
  <li>even help smart speakers in homes and voice assistants in cars understand different accents.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>9. According to the text, the majority of English speakers…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) have an American accent.</span>
  <span>(b) live in the US.</span>
  <span>(c) don’t have English as their first language.</span>
  <span>(d) use apps to help their pronunciation.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) don’t have English as their first language.</strong></p>
  <p>La mayoría de quienes hablan inglés no son nativos (líneas 1-2): <em>“An estimated 1.5 billion people speak it, and most of them aren’t native speakers.”</em></p>
  <p>El texto no dice que la mayoría tenga acento estadounidense (a) ni que viva en EE. UU. (b); y la aplicación de Sanas todavía está en pruebas (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>10. According to the text, Sanas…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) takes too long to process the change of accent.</span>
  <span>(b) intends to improve communication.</span>
  <span>(c) is not based on artificial intelligence.</span>
  <span>(d) translates from one language to another.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) intends to improve communication.</strong></p>
  <p>Su objetivo es acabar con los malentendidos (línea 3): <em>“software that aims to eliminate miscommunication”</em>.</p>
  <p>El cambio es casi instantáneo (<em>“nearly instantaneous”</em>) (a); funciona con inteligencia artificial (<em>“artificial intelligence-powered”</em>) (c); y cambia el acento, no traduce entre idiomas (d).</p>
</Solucion>
