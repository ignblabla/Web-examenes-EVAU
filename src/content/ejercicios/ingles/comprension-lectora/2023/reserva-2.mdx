<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: THE HISTORY OF TATTOOS</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">In 1991 two Germans hiking in the Alps found by chance what they initially believed to be a modern corpse frozen in the ice.</li>
  <li>Later, authorities discovered that it was anything but modern. The mummy, nicknamed Ötzi after the valley where it lay, had</li>
  <li>survived in the ice for 5,300 years. Mystery surrounds the exact circumstances of Ötzi’s death although data suggest a violent end.</li>
  <li class="is-start">However, this isn’t the only secret Ötzi hides. He has over fifty lines and crosses tattooed onto his body —the earliest known</li>
  <li>evidence of tattooing in the world. The locations of the markings are consistent with traditional Chinese acupuncture points. So,</li>
  <li>Ötzi’s tattoos probably played a therapeutic role.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Before Ötzi, the first conclusive evidence of tattoos came from a handful of Egyptian mummies dating back to the construction</li>
  <li>of the great pyramids, over 4,000 years ago. Historical texts reveal that tattooing has been practiced by almost every human</li>
  <li>culture. For instance, the ancient Greeks used tattoos to communicate among spies. Later, the Romans marked criminals and</li>
  <li>slaves with tattoos. During the crusades, Europeans tattooed a cross on their hands or arms to show that they had participated in</li>
  <li>the war and indicate their desire for a Christian burial.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The popularity of tattoos has risen and fallen over time. Currently, the practice is booming. The reasons for getting tattoos vary:</li>
  <li>religion, status or artistic expression. Now, a new one can be added: a webpage designer recently sold his forehead as advertising</li>
  <li>space on eBay. He got &#36;37,000 in return for having a pharmaceutical brand logo tattooed for one month. If Ötzi weren’t in deep</li>
  <li>freeze at a museum, I’m sure he’d be rolling in his grave.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>1. According to the text, tattooing…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) is done for different purposes.</span>
  <span>(b) has always been trendy.</span>
  <span>(c) gave rise to acupuncture.</span>
  <span>(d) is not documented in Ancient Egypt.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(a) is done for different purposes.</strong></p>
  <p>La gente se tatúa por muchos motivos (líneas 12-13): <em>“The reasons for getting tattoos vary: religion, status or artistic expression.”</em> El texto menciona además usos terapéuticos, de espionaje o para marcar a delincuentes.</p>
  <p>Su popularidad ha subido y bajado con el tiempo (<em>“has risen and fallen over time”</em>) (b); los tatuajes de Ötzi coinciden con puntos de acupuntura, pero no la originaron (c); y hay momias egipcias tatuadas (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>2. According to the text, eBay…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) has hired a website designer to advertise tattoos.</span>
  <span>(b) has put Ötzi’s body up for sale.</span>
  <span>(c) has made it possible to use a body part for publicity.</span>
  <span>(d) is selling pharmaceutical products to make tattoos.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) has made it possible to use a body part for publicity.</strong></p>
  <p>Un diseñador vendió su frente en eBay como espacio publicitario (líneas 13-14): <em>“a webpage designer recently sold his forehead as advertising space on eBay.”</em></p>
  <p>Fue el diseñador quien usó eBay, no eBay quien lo contrató (a); Ötzi está en un museo (b); y lo que se tatuó fue el logo de una marca farmacéutica, no productos para tatuar (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: ALL UK NUMBER 1 HITS</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">The UK singles chart, a weekly countdown of the most popular songs on the radio, has been a constant in our lives for seventy</li>
  <li>years. But to Dave Watson, it’s a lifestyle. Watson has been collecting copies of UK Number 1 hits since the late 1980s; today, he</li>
  <li>owns all 1,404 UK Number 1 singles, reaching back to the birth of the charts in 1952. He believes it’s the only complete collection</li>
  <li>of its kind.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Watson’s devotion to music charts began more than thirty years ago. Starting a collection made sense: his mother had worked</li>
  <li>in a record store, and he enjoyed collecting things. When he started his mission in 1988, the charts had already seen 605 Number</li>
  <li>1 singles. With the single “Don’t Turn Around” under his arm, he decided to find the previous 604 releases.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In his treasure hunt, Watson used to get trains to London to visit music fairs and second-hand record shops. He also responded</li>
  <li>to selling adverts in <em>Time Out</em> and <em>Loot</em> magazines and wrote to record dealers. “I spent endless hours searching through dealers’</li>
  <li>stock,” he says. “I had a handwritten list that I photocopied and took around with me to spread the word. Some sellers answered</li>
  <li>back saying which singles they had and how much they would be.” His first wish list was already several pages long. Then he</li>
  <li>started crossing titles out and he told himself, “Well, I have a chance of doing all this.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">Discovering eBay around the millennium was a turning point. With a mouse click, Watson found there the last ten singles he</li>
  <li>needed. With the 1953 single “Doggy in the Window” Watson brought the collection up to date.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>9. According to the text, the UK singles chart started…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) in the 1950s.</span>
  <span>(b) 30 years ago.</span>
  <span>(c) in the 1970s.</span>
  <span>(d) in the late 1980s.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(a) in the 1950s.</strong></p>
  <p>La lista nació en 1952 (línea 3): <em>“reaching back to the birth of the charts in 1952.”</em></p>
  <p>Hace 30 años y a finales de los 80 es cuando Watson empezó su colección (b y d); y el texto no menciona los años 70 (c).</p>
</Solucion>

</Solucion>
