<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: IT’S NOT EASY TO BE GREEN</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">“I’ll try to eat less meat,” you think, as you pick up a plant-based alternative to your usual beef burger. Then you discover it’s</li>
  <li>almost double the price of the meaty version on the next shop shelf. “Sorry, meat wins.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">Supermarkets have expanded the range of meat-alternative products in the last five years, with a variety of items that would</li>
  <li>tempt any carnivore. There’s one problem, though: the price.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Plant-based options often cost more than regular meat products. Take <em>Bird’s Eye</em> nuggets as an example. A standard packet</li>
  <li>costs £1.55 at <em>Tesco</em>; the brand’s vegan choice is £2.50. So, why are they so pricey? Plant-based alternatives are a relatively new</li>
  <li>and developing part of the market, which means more money is spent on research and innovation to ensure they meet the demand</li>
  <li>for taste and quality. The good news is that the market is moving in the right direction. In December 2020, <em>Plant &amp; Bean</em> opened</li>
  <li>Europe’s largest plant-based meat production facility in the UK to help drive down prices.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Supermarkets are also making changes. In April, <em>Co-Op</em> invested more than £1.7 million to reduce the cost of vegan food. As</li>
  <li>part of the move, its own-brand vegan sausages fell from £3 to £1.45. No ingredients were changed.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Until prices go down, the Vegan Society says it’s still possible to embrace a vegan lifestyle by choosing other products. Actually,</li>
  <li>it’s a misconception that vegan food is necessarily more expensive than animal products since some of the cheapest key</li>
  <li>components of a vegan diet are rice, pasta, vegetables and beans.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The Vegan Society hopes to increase access to affordable plant-based food and support vegans who find themselves in</li>
  <li>financial difficulty.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>1. According to the text, vegan products are…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) tasteless for any carnivore.</span>
  <span>(b) often cheaper than meat products.</span>
  <span>(c) appealing to non-vegan people too.</span>
  <span>(d) hard to find.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) appealing to non-vegan people too.</strong></p>
  <p>Los supermercados ofrecen alternativas a la carne que tentarían incluso a quien come carne (líneas 3-4): <em>“a variety of items that would tempt any carnivore.”</em></p>
  <p>Es lo contrario: resultan atractivos incluso para los carnívoros (a); suelen ser más caros, no más baratos (<em>“Plant-based options often cost more than regular meat products”</em>) (b); y los supermercados han ampliado su oferta, así que no son difíciles de encontrar (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>2. According to the text, customers…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) can’t find meat alternative products in the supermarket.</span>
  <span>(b) can still be green in spite of the high prices.</span>
  <span>(c) can afford any product.</span>
  <span>(d) choose the vegan alternative instead of <em>Bird’s Eye</em> nuggets.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) can still be green in spite of the high prices.</strong></p>
  <p>Mientras los precios no bajen, se puede llevar una dieta vegana eligiendo otros productos más baratos (línea 12): <em>“Until prices go down, the Vegan Society says it’s still possible to embrace a vegan lifestyle by choosing other products.”</em> Por ejemplo, arroz, pasta, verduras y legumbres.</p>
  <p>Sí encuentran estos productos, porque la oferta ha crecido (a); el precio es precisamente el problema (<em>“There’s one problem, though: the price”</em>) (c); y el texto no dice que elijan la versión vegana de los <em>nuggets</em>; de hecho, el ejemplo inicial muestra que el precio lleva a elegir la carne (<em>“Sorry, meat wins”</em>) (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: MEN EARN MORE MONEY THAN WOMEN IN EVERY COUNTRY</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">A new international study examining the difference in earnings within couples has revealed that men continue to be paid more</li>
  <li>than women in every country.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In the first survey of its kind, scholars examined public data from 2.85 million households across 45 countries to reach their</li>
  <li>conclusions. The report, published in the <em>Journal of Economic Behavior &amp; Organization</em>, analysed the income of couples between</li>
  <li>the ages of 18 and 65 over a four-decade period. The team wanted to assess the global picture, particularly in Scandinavian</li>
  <li>countries, which are often presented as more progressive for females. However, the experts concluded that gender pay inequality</li>
  <li>persists across countries, over time and across both rich and poor households. They found that the family unit is often a place of</li>
  <li>great inequality, even in the most affluent nations.</li>
  <li class="is-start">One positive finding was that women’s participation in the labour market had increased over the 40-year period. In many parts</li>
  <li>of the world, more women-friendly policies have narrowed the gap. Nevertheless, the researchers felt that governments were not</li>
  <li>doing enough.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In May 2020, on the fiftieth anniversary of the Equal Pay Act, the Trade Unions Congress (TUC) stated that women continue</li>
  <li>to be underpaid and undervalued. General secretary of the TUC, Frances O’Grady, said: “Fifty years after brave women won the</li>
  <li>legal right to equal pay, unequal earnings are still common today. Working women are stuck in low-paid and insecure jobs. No</li>
  <li>doubt we need to reconsider how we value and reward their work. Without appropriate measures, it will take decades to close the</li>
  <li>gender pay gap.”</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>9. According to the text and in order to conduct the study, the researchers...</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) visited different households.</span>
  <span>(b) focused on single-parent families.</span>
  <span>(c) questioned family units.</span>
  <span>(d) analysed accessible information.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(d) analysed accessible information.</strong></p>
  <p>Los investigadores trabajaron con datos públicos (línea 3): <em>“scholars examined public data from 2.85 million households across 45 countries”</em>.</p>
  <p>No visitaron hogares (a) ni entrevistaron a las familias (c), sino que analizaron datos ya disponibles; y estudiaron parejas (<em>“the income of couples”</em>), no familias monoparentales (b).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>10. According to the text, the labour world has seen how women…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) have intensified their presence in recent decades.</span>
  <span>(b) over 40 years old are generally unemployed.</span>
  <span>(c) have reduced the number of jobs available.</span>
  <span>(d) are not prepared to fulfill some tasks.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(a) have intensified their presence in recent decades.</strong></p>
  <p>La participación de las mujeres en el mercado laboral aumentó durante los 40 años estudiados (línea 9): <em>“women’s participation in the labour market had increased over the 40-year period.”</em></p>
  <p>Los 40 años se refieren al periodo del estudio, no a la edad de las mujeres (b); y el texto no dice nada de que hayan reducido los empleos (c) ni de que no estén preparadas para algunas tareas (d).</p>
</Solucion>
