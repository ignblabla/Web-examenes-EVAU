<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: JOSÉ ADOLFO QUISOCALA IS CHANGING THE WORLD</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">When he was seven, José Adolfo Quisocala created a bank for school friends in his hometown of Arequipa, Peru, to save</li>
  <li>money to buy books, stationery and uniforms. Now, nine years later, the Bartselana Student Bank has 6,700 customers, all aged</li>
  <li>under 18. All these children can add more money to their savings by taking plastic and paper to recycle to school bus stops —the</li>
  <li>money is automatically credited to their bank accounts. Every month, the bank recycles between 15 and 16 tons of paper and</li>
  <li>plastic.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The idea came to Quisocala when he saw children begging at traffic lights and wondered how he could help them and their</li>
  <li>families earn and save, and at the same time recycle household rubbish that would otherwise become litter. He left school to</li>
  <li>pursue his idea, but it was worth it, he says. “In my town, I considerably reduced the level of child poverty, the school drop-out rate</li>
  <li>and environmental pollution.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">Quisocala also helped create the Bartselana Foundation, which turns donations of recyclable waste from local companies into</li>
  <li>funds to combat child hunger and improve education. The foundation has been making free online educational videos lately, which</li>
  <li>they put out via social media, explaining how to distinguish different types of plastic and paper for recycling, for example. “This is</li>
  <li>relevant information which is not taught at school so we make it public and free to watch,” says Quisocala.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Now 16, he plans to pass the bank to another young executive in order to go to university. He intends to continue his social</li>
  <li>activism, working with the “least-favoured or forgotten groups in Latin America” and helping them benefit from carrying out</li>
  <li>environmental services.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>1. José Adolfo started the bank…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) when he was in his teens.</span>
  <span>(b) for every young student in Arequipa.</span>
  <span>(c) to profit from his friends’ money.</span>
  <span>(d) to help his friends pay for their education.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(d) to help his friends pay for their education.</strong></p>
  <p>Creó el banco para que sus compañeros ahorraran y pudieran comprar libros, material escolar y uniformes (líneas 1-2): <em>“created a bank for school friends… to save money to buy books, stationery and uniforms.”</em></p>
  <p>Lo creó con siete años, no en la adolescencia (a); era para sus compañeros de colegio, no para todos los estudiantes de Arequipa (b); y su objetivo era ayudar, no sacar beneficio (c).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>2. The Bartselana Student Bank has been operating…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">

  <span>(b) only for minors.</span>
  <span>(c) for all people in Arequipa who wish to save money.</span>
  <span>(d) at José Adolfo’s Primary School exclusively.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) only for minors.</strong></p>
  <p>Todos sus clientes son menores de 18 años (líneas 2-3): <em>“the Bartselana Student Bank has 6,700 customers, all aged under 18.”</em></p>
  <p>Funciona desde hace nueve años, no más de diez (<em>“Now, nine years later”</em>) (a); solo pueden usarlo menores, no cualquier persona de Arequipa (c); y con 6.700 clientes y paradas de autobús escolar como puntos de reciclaje, no se limita a su colegio (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: GARUM IN THE 21<sup>st</sup> CENTURY</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">In modern laboratories, scientists are working to recreate <em>garum</em>, a sauce made from putrefying fish that was beloved by the</li>
  <li>ancient Greeks and Romans but hardly sounds appealing to 21st-century food lovers.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The Latin term <em>garum</em> could refer to both a sauce used in the cooking process —sometimes also called <em>liquamen</em>— and to a</li>
  <li>condiment, made with the blood and viscera of fish, that writers such as Petronius, Ausonius and Seneca knew as <em>garum sociorum</em></li>
  <li>(“garum of the allies”).</li>
  <li class="is-start">Archaeologists had been unsuccessful in extracting well-preserved <em>garum</em> at excavations in Tunisia and other Mediterranean</li>
  <li>locations until Italian researchers in 2009 discovered six sealed <em>dolia</em> —large clay amphoras— in a building that they named the</li>
  <li>Garum Shop at Pompeii. The eruption of Mount Vesuvius in AD 79 had buried the building under ash, thus perfectly preserving a</li>
  <li>small <em>liquamen</em> factory.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Food technicians from the universities of Cadiz and Seville analysed the contents of the <em>dolia</em> at Pompeii and, using that</li>
  <li>information and a <em>liquamen</em> recipe thought to have been written in the third century AD, produced what they claim is the first</li>
  <li>scientific recreation of the 2,000-year-old fish sauce. This recreation is called <em>Flor de Garum</em> and is being sold in amphora-shaped</li>
  <li>glass bottles throughout Spain. Chefs state that it supercharges dishes with a potent flavour.</li>
  <li class="is-start">It has been said that it is impossible to recreate definitive modern versions of these Roman ancient fish sauces, but at least</li>
  <li>this is the first time in modern history that a scientific reconstruction of <em>garum</em> has been successfully achieved.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>9. According to the text, …</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) <em>garum</em> was a popular sweet in ancient times.</span>
  <span>(b) the <em>garum</em> sauce was cooked with ashes.</span>
  <span>(c) it is now possible to buy a version of <em>garum</em>.</span>
  <span>(d) <em>garum</em> was made with fresh fish.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) it is now possible to buy a version of <em>garum</em>.</strong></p>
  <p>La recreación, llamada <em>Flor de Garum</em>, está a la venta en toda España (líneas 12-13): <em>“This recreation is called Flor de Garum and is being sold in amphora-shaped glass bottles throughout Spain.”</em></p>
  <p>El <em>garum</em> era una salsa de pescado, no un dulce (a); la ceniza del Vesubio sepultó el edificio, no se usaba para cocinar la salsa (b); y se hacía con pescado en descomposición (<em>“putrefying fish”</em>), no fresco (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>10. According to the text, <em>Flor de Garum</em> is made using…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) a recipe collected by Seneca and other classical writers.</span>
  <span>(b) 2,000-year-old fish.</span>
  <span>(c) data obtained from Pompeian <em>dolia</em>.</span>
  <span>(d) information gathered from excavations in Tunisia.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) data obtained from Pompeian <em>dolia</em>.</strong></p>
  <p>Los técnicos analizaron el contenido de las ánforas de Pompeya y usaron esa información para recrear la salsa (líneas 10-12): <em>“Food technicians… analysed the contents of the dolia at Pompeii and, using that information and a liquamen recipe…, produced what they claim is the first scientific recreation”</em>.</p>
  <p>La receta que usaron es del siglo III d. C., no de Séneca, que solo aparece como autor que conocía el condimento (a); lo que tiene 2.000 años es la salsa original, no el pescado que se usa ahora (b); y en Túnez no se consiguió extraer <em>garum</em> bien conservado (d).</p>
</Solucion>
