<p class="en-heading">BEYOND THE STARS</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">There is more than one way to lead a successful life. Ronald McNair succeeded despite racism. He became an accidental</li>
  <li>revolutionary as a kid, when he broke the color barrier in Lake City, South Carolina. His act of rebelliousness would set the course</li>
  <li>for his relatively short but full life, which culminated in his career as an astronaut.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Ronald McNair was born in 1950. Although the family was poor, Ron grew up surrounded by books, music, and the strong</li>
  <li>support of his mother, who nurtured his love of reading. From a young age, he was obsessed with space.</li>
  <li class="is-start">When Ron first went into the public library in 1959, he found out that the library was segregated and that, unlike white boys,</li>
  <li>he wasn’t allowed to check a book out. Nine-year-old Ron didn’t give up: He simply refused to leave the library without his science</li>
  <li>books. The librarian called the cops, who immediately called his mother. Pearl McNair proudly stood by her son in the matter,</li>
  <li>persuading the librarian to let him borrow the books. From that day on, Ron was allowed to check out books whenever he wanted.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Despite his high grades, Ron couldn’t just apply to any college he liked. After earning his degree from the nearest college</li>
  <li>offering physics to Afro-American students, Ron got a brochure in the mail from NASA one day. They were looking for mission</li>
  <li>specialist candidates. He enthusiastically applied and was accepted from a pool of 10,000 applicants.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Ron spent six years in astronaut training before his first mission on the Space Shuttle Challenger in 1984. The mission’s goal</li>
  <li>was to put a satellite into orbit, but Ron had another, secret objective: playing the saxophone in space. By blowing a solo</li>
  <li>performance of “What the World Needs Now Is Love,” Ron McNair became the first person to play a saxophone in space.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Two years later, Ron was scheduled to return to space on the unfortunate 1986 Challenger mission. He lost his life along with</li>
  <li>six other crew members when the shuttle broke apart during launch on January 28th.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Since the tragedy, many institutional buildings have been named in honor of the seven men and women whose lives were cut</li>
  <li>short that day. In 2011, Lake City Library was turned into Ronald E. McNair Life History Center. Anywhere a kid can be found trying</li>
  <li>to rise above their circumstances, Ron’s spirit lives on.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>A1. According to the text, after the incident at the local library, Ron…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) was reprimanded by his mother.</span>
  <span>(b) had to get science books elsewhere.</span>
  <span>(c) could become a regular borrower.</span>
  <span>(d) had to resign himself to attending black libraries.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) could become a regular borrower.</strong></p>
  <p>Desde aquel día pudo sacar libros siempre que quiso (línea 9): <em>“From that day on, Ron was allowed to check out books whenever he wanted.”</em></p>
  <p>Su madre no lo regañó, sino que lo apoyó con orgullo (<em>“proudly stood by her son”</em>) (a); consiguió los libros en esa misma biblioteca (b); y pudo seguir usándola, sin tener que ir a bibliotecas solo para negros (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>A2. According to the text, Ron…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) picked his university after receiving a brochure.</span>
  <span>(b) got an excellent academic record.</span>
  <span>(c) was encouraged to become an astronaut by his mother.</span>
  <span>(d) was the first black astronaut.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) got an excellent academic record.</strong></p>
  <p>Sacaba muy buenas notas (línea 10): <em>“Despite his high grades, Ron couldn’t just apply to any college he liked.”</em></p>
  <p>El folleto de la NASA le llegó después de terminar la carrera, no antes de elegir universidad (a); su madre fomentó su amor por la lectura, pero el interés por el espacio era suyo (c); y el texto dice que fue el primero en tocar el saxofón en el espacio, no el primer astronauta negro (d).</p>
</Solucion>
