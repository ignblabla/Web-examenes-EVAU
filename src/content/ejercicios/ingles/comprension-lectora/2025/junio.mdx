<p class="en-heading">DOLLAR PRINCESSES</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">During the late 1800s, a period also known as The Gilded Age, the US experienced rapid economic growth following the Civil</li>
  <li>War. Although some American families became insanely wealthy, they still considered themselves “commoners”, lacking the</li>
  <li>exquisite titles of European aristocracy, such as Dukes, Barons, and the like. This became unacceptable to many of them who</li>
  <li>wanted an aristocratic identity to accompany their newfound fortunes.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Meanwhile, British noblemen represented “old world charm” on the other side of the Atlantic. They were sophisticated, cultured,</li>
  <li>and aristocratic, with noble titles passed down through their family legacies. However, they were desperate for cash due to</li>
  <li>economic pressures mostly caused by rural populations moving to the cities and the costly maintenance of their aging mansions.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The answer to the problems of the American millionaires and the British aristocracy can be summed up in two words: dollar</li>
  <li>princesses. <em>Dollar Princess</em> was a term referring to an American heiress, a woman from a newly wealthy family. The fathers of</li>
  <li>these “princesses” believed that a title would provide a shortcut to social acceptance. Since the American Constitution forbade the</li>
  <li>government from giving out titles of nobility, they “sold” their daughters to British aristocrats in exchange for titles.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In a short time, more than &#36;25 billion was sent to Britain, rejuvenating dying aristocratic families and boosting the European</li>
  <li>economy. If these marriages seem like cold, hard contractual negotiations, it’s because they were. Very often, the future husbands</li>
  <li>were complete strangers to the young heiresses, who led hopeless and miserable lives with no support.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Once in Britain, most dollar princesses found their majestic homes terribly dark and cold, as they were in desperate need of</li>
  <li>repair. Soon, the new wives remodeled their homes, but they were often judged, and even mocked, for doing so. To make matters</li>
  <li>worse, they were marginalized by high society for their non-aristocratic roots.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In 1880, railway heiress Frances Ellen Work married the future Baron Fermoy. Like many dollar-princess matches, it was an</li>
  <li>unhappy one. The baron blew Frances’s &#36;2.5 million fortune on gambling, and the couple divorced in 1891. Frances’s story might</li>
  <li>suggest that dollar princesses were but an anecdote in the history of Britain. However, just over a century after she traded her</li>
  <li>money for aristocracy, her great-granddaughter Diana Spencer became the Princess of Wales—and later, the mother of Prince</li>
  <li>William, now first in line to the British throne.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>A1. According to the text, Dollar Princesses…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) married against their parents’ wishes.</span>
  <span>(b) saved their parents’ fortunes.</span>
  <span>(c) challenged their parents’ authority.</span>
  <span>(d) improved their parents’ prestige in social circles.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(d) improved their parents’ prestige in social circles.</strong></p>
  <p>Los padres casaban a sus hijas con aristócratas británicos porque creían que un título era un atajo para ser aceptados socialmente (líneas 9-10): <em>“The fathers of these ‘princesses’ believed that a title would provide a shortcut to social acceptance.”</em></p>
  <p>No se casaron en contra de la voluntad de sus padres ni desafiaron su autoridad, sino que fueron ellos quienes las “vendieron” (<em>“they ‘sold’ their daughters”</em>) (a y c); y el dinero no salvó la fortuna de sus padres, sino la de las familias aristocráticas británicas (<em>“rejuvenating dying aristocratic families”</em>) (b).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>A2. According to the text, after the wedding, Dollar Princesses…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) were made fun of by their husbands.</span>
  <span>(b) were entirely on their own.</span>
  <span>(c) used their fortunes to entertain themselves.</span>
  <span>(d) were held in high regard by the noble class.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) were entirely on their own.</strong></p>
  <p>Llevaban vidas desesperanzadas y desgraciadas, sin ningún apoyo (línea 14): <em>“the young heiresses, who led hopeless and miserable lives with no support.”</em></p>
  <p>El texto dice que se burlaban de ellas (<em>“mocked”</em>), pero no que lo hicieran sus maridos (a); emplearon su dinero en reformar las casas, no en divertirse (c); y la alta sociedad las marginaba (<em>“they were marginalized by high society”</em>) (d).</p>
</Solucion>
