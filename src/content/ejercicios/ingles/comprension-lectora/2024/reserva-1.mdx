<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: LA CATRINA</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">Mexicans have an exceptional relationship with death and the afterlife because they are quite unique in taking it good-</li>
  <li>humoredly and passionately. That doesn’t mean that Mexicans don’t grieve or miss those who have passed away —they just</li>
  <li>choose to remember them by celebrating their lives and memories. On November 2nd, the Day of the Dead, families gather in</li>
  <li>cemeteries to feast while they clean tombstones, sing songs, and talk to their ancestors. Parents might even introduce a baby to</li>
  <li>a grandparent who died before the baby was born.</li>
  <li class="is-start">One of the most recognizable symbols of the Day of the Dead celebrations is La Catrina, a female skeleton wearing a fancy</li>
  <li>hat. Her striking makeup has become trendy worldwide. The essence of La Catrina’s story goes deep into Mexican roots as the</li>
  <li>Aztecs worshipped Mictecacihuatl, a goddess of death that protected and helped their departed loved ones into the next stages.</li>
  <li>However, the skull that we see today was created in the early 1900s by José Guadalupe Posada. This cartoonist drew <em>calaveras</em></li>
  <li>in a satirical way to urge people to stop pretending to be something that they weren’t, for they would all die someday.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Later, famous artist and husband of Frida Kahlo, Diego Rivera, immortalized Posada’s Catrina in one of his murals. He painted</li>
  <li>her wearing sophisticated clothing and an extravagant hat with feathers, thus creating the multicolored look that she’s well-known</li>
  <li>for today. From there, La Catrina became a powerful symbol for the numerous Day of the Dead events. Women use colorful</li>
  <li>makeup and wear lively outfits evoking the famous skeleton.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Tourists find La Catrina fascinating and many purchase Catrina figurines as souvenirs. La Catrina is definitely a Mexican icon!</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>1. According to the text, …</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) Mexicans don’t have a frivolous attitude towards death.</span>
  <span>(b) the modern image of La Catrina looks like an Aztec goddess.</span>
  <span>(c) Rivera turned his wife into the famous icon of La Catrina.</span>
  <span>(d) Frida Kahlo designed La Catrina’s costume.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(a) Mexicans don’t have a frivolous attitude towards death.</strong></p>
  <p>Aunque celebran a sus muertos con alegría, eso no significa que no sufran su pérdida (línea 2): <em>“That doesn’t mean that Mexicans don’t grieve or miss those who have passed away”</em>.</p>
  <p>La imagen actual de La Catrina la creó Posada a principios del siglo XX, no es la diosa azteca (b); Rivera pintó la Catrina de Posada, no a su mujer (c); y Frida Kahlo solo aparece como esposa de Rivera, no como diseñadora (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>2. According to the text, Posada…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) came up with the idea of the Day of the Dead.</span>
  <span>(b) was a makeup artist.</span>
  <span>(c) reminded people of the certainty of death.</span>
  <span>(d) portrayed death in stylish clothes.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) reminded people of the certainty of death.</strong></p>
  <p>Dibujaba calaveras para recordar a la gente que todos acabarían muriendo (línea 10): <em>“to urge people to stop pretending to be something that they weren’t, for they would all die someday.”</em></p>
  <p>El Día de Muertos tiene raíces mucho más antiguas (a); Posada era caricaturista (<em>“This cartoonist”</em>), no maquillador (b); y fue Rivera quien la pintó con ropa elegante (<em>“sophisticated clothing”</em>) (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: THE DISAPPEARANCE OF MICHAEL ROCKEFELLER</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">Michael Rockefeller, great-grandson of the founder of the wealthy Rockefeller dynasty, was deeply impressed when his father</li>
  <li>opened the Museum of Primitive Art in 1960. He decided there and then to devote his life to finding “primitive” art —a term no</li>
  <li>longer used today due to its implications that non-Western cultures are inferior. Within months of the museum launch, Michael</li>
  <li>went on an expedition trip to New Guinea —ruled by the Netherlands at the time— to collect art from the native Asmat people.</li>
  <li>Michael learned on arrival that Asmat warriors took the heads of their enemies in battle and that they practised cannibalism. He</li>
  <li>was fascinated by these people, who represented such an intriguing violation of the norms of Western society.</li>
  <li class="is-start">When Michael returned to the island one year later, his boat sank. The Rockefellers spent a small fortune supporting the</li>
  <li>Dutch’s search for Michael. Despite all efforts, the search was called off barely two weeks after the boating accident. Michael was</li>
  <li>never seen again. The official cause of death was filed as drowning, but the news and rumours about it spread like wildfire in</li>
  <li>tabloids and newspapers.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In 2014, reporter Carl Hoffman revealed in a book that the Netherlands’ inquiries into the matter had concluded, shortly after</li>
  <li>the disappearance, that the Asmat had killed Michael. But why would the Dutch bury those files? According to Hoffman, Michael</li>
  <li>couldn’t have arrived at a worst time: the Netherlands was losing the colony to Indonesia, and the scandal would have exposed</li>
  <li>their lack of control. So, they denied the murder rumours even to the Rockefellers, who to this day refuse to accept any other</li>
  <li>version of events than that Michael drowned.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>9. According to the text, Michael Rockefeller travelled to New Guinea…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) once, and never returned from there.</span>
  <span>(b) because he wanted to found a new museum.</span>
  <span>(c) in search of articles of local art.</span>
  <span>(d) after its independence from the Netherlands.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) in search of articles of local art.</strong></p>
  <p>Viajó para reunir obras de arte de los asmat (línea 4): <em>“to collect art from the native Asmat people.”</em></p>
  <p>Fue dos veces: volvió a la isla un año después (<em>“When Michael returned to the island one year later”</em>) (a); el museo ya lo había abierto su padre (b); y entonces la isla seguía gobernada por los Países Bajos (<em>“ruled by the Netherlands at the time”</em>) (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>10. According to the text, the Rockefellers…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) assigned Michael the hunt for museum pieces.</span>
  <span>(b) still don’t believe that Michael drowned.</span>
  <span>(c) were lied to about Michael’s death at the time.</span>
  <span>(d) searched for Michael on their own.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) were lied to about Michael’s death at the time.</strong></p>
  <p>Los neerlandeses les ocultaron que los asmat lo habían matado (línea 14): <em>“they denied the murder rumours even to the Rockefellers”</em>.</p>
  <p>La decisión de buscar arte fue del propio Michael (<em>“He decided there and then”</em>) (a); la familia sigue creyendo que se ahogó (b); y financiaron la búsqueda de los neerlandeses, no la hicieron solos (d).</p>
</Solucion>
