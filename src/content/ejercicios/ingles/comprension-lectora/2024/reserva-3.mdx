<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: DAYLIGHT SAVING TIME</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">Spring marks many changes and, for many people around the world, the beginning of Daylight Saving Time (DST). The idea</li>
  <li>behind the clock shift is to make better use of sunlight.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Benjamin Franklin is often credited as the first to suggest the change in 1784. When he was an American delegate in Paris,</li>
  <li>he felt he was wasting his Parisian mornings by staying in bed after the sun had come up. He ironically proposed that the French</li>
  <li>should fire cannons at sunrise to wake up people, so that they would go to bed earlier and use fewer candles at night.</li>
  <li class="is-start">It wasn’t until resources became limited during World War I that Germany decided to go ahead with a clock-changing plan,</li>
  <li>implementing the first DST in 1916. Most of Europe followed their example in a matter of weeks, and the United States did the</li>
  <li>same in 1918.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Nowadays, DST still has fervent supporters who claim that it helps drive the economy. Conversely, it’s often argued that, apart</li>
  <li>from the legion of missed meetings and sleepy citizens, DST causes adverse effects on our health and safety. Studies reveal that</li>
  <li>changing clocks brings about an increase in heart attacks and fatal car accidents. DST opponents even question whether there</li>
  <li>have ever been energy savings at all.</li>
  <li class="is-start">All in all, the time shift is becoming more and more unpopular around the world, since people today are fond of going out after</li>
  <li>work and it’s obviously nicer doing so when it’s lighter outside. Recently, a survey showed 80% of Europeans were against DST,</li>
  <li>and every October the matter is a controversial topic, with people either expressing enthusiasm about sunny mornings or</li>
  <li>complaining about shorter days.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: JUNETEENTH</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">On June 17<sup>th</sup>, 2021, President Biden signed into law the bill that established as a national public holiday June 19<sup>th</sup>, Juneteenth</li>
  <li>National Independence Day. Despite Juneteenth’s storied history, the holiday had largely gone unnoticed by non-Black Americans</li>
  <li>for long. It was the Black Lives Matter movement that pushed Juneteenth into the national spotlight, following decades of activists’</li>
  <li>calls for recognition of the landmark occasion.</li>
  <li class="is-start">In 1863, during the American Civil War between the Union and the Confederacy, President Lincoln issued the Emancipation</li>
  <li>Proclamation, which declared free more than three million slaves living in the Confederate States. More than two years would</li>
  <li>pass, however, before the news reached African Americans living in Texas. It was not until Union soldiers arrived in Galveston,</li>
  <li>Texas, on June 19<sup>th</sup>, 1865 that the town’s residents finally learned that slavery had been abolished. The former slaves immediately</li>
  <li>began to celebrate with prayer, feasting, song and dance.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The following year, on June 19<sup>th</sup>, the first official Juneteenth celebrations took place in Texas. They included prayer meetings</li>
  <li>and the singing of spirituals, and celebrants wore new clothes as a way of representing their newfound freedom.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Since African Americans were prohibited from using public facilities, they often held their annual celebration of freedom at their</li>
  <li>churches or out in rural areas around rivers and streams, which could provide for additional activities such as fishing, horseback riding</li>
  <li>and barbecues. There were also visits to the cemeteries to reunite the freed and living with their dead and enslaved ancestors.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Having been commemorated for more than 160 years, Juneteenth has now become a date representing African Americans’</li>
  <li>struggle for freedom and equality, as well as an occasion for family and community celebration.</li>
</ol>
