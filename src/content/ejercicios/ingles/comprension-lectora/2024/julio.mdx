<p class="en-heading">TEXT 1: THE ETHICS OF TRUE CRIME</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">True crime TV shows have been so popular in the past few years that they have become the object of academic study.</li>
  <li>Professor of Media Ethics Whitney Phillips claims that the reason for the current success of this genre, as compared to novels and</li>
  <li>movies, is the reality factor: audiences enjoy feeling engaged in an actual mystery happening before them.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Research has found that white women are the largest demographic group consuming true crime. The explanation is that, since</li>
  <li>white women have anxiety about potential threats, they turn to true crime to feel better prepared in case anything violent were to</li>
  <li>happen to them. The fact that the target audience is basically white women determines the profile of typical victims in true crime</li>
  <li>—white and female. After all, audiences prefer to watch cases they can relate to. Phillips argues that this produces the “missing</li>
  <li>white woman syndrome,” that is, the idea that there’s only one type of victim whose story matters. The media reinforce this with</li>
  <li>many stories and updates about white female victims, while stories about marginalized groups like Indigenous women are rarely</li>
  <li>shown, if at all. Even white women who are missing or murdered receive little media attention when they’re sex workers, because</li>
  <li>they’re perceived as less valuable than “innocent” white women.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Another harmful effect resulting from the boom of true crime is the dehumanization of victims when their tragedies become</li>
  <li>marketable content. As one victim said, “My trauma’s not your entertainment. Awareness is a whole different story.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">Fortunately, there’s an alternative: ethical true crime, which avoids sensationalising the narrative and honours victims. Further</li>
  <li>still, it seeks a way to help their case and alert to social inequalities.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>1. According to the text, the key to the boom of true crime shows lies in…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) adapting popular books and films.</span>
  <span>(b) presenting real people’s cases.</span>
  <span>(c) dealing with different types of victims.</span>
  <span>(d) its academic research interest.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) presenting real people’s cases.</strong></p>
  <p>El éxito del género se debe a que los casos son reales (líneas 2-3): <em>“the reason for the current success of this genre… is the reality factor: audiences enjoy feeling engaged in an actual mystery happening before them.”</em></p>
  <p>El texto compara el género con las novelas y películas, pero no dice que las adapte (a); muestra casi siempre un único tipo de víctima, mujeres blancas (c); y el interés académico es una consecuencia de su éxito, no la causa (<em>“so popular… that they have become the object of academic study”</em>) (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>2. According to the text, white women follow the true crime genre in great numbers…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) because it is a brand-new genre.</span>
  <span>(b) because they like thrilling stories.</span>
  <span>(c) to learn how to keep safe.</span>
  <span>(d) out of sympathy for marginalised victims.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(c) to learn how to keep safe.</strong></p>
  <p>Lo ven para sentirse mejor preparadas por si les ocurre algo violento (líneas 4-6): <em>“since white women have anxiety about potential threats, they turn to true crime to feel better prepared in case anything violent were to happen to them.”</em></p>
  <p>El texto no dice que el género sea nuevo (a) ni que lo vean por la emoción de las historias (b); y prefieren casos con los que se identifican, mientras que las víctimas marginadas apenas aparecen (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: POMFRET, VERMONT: THE TOWN THAT WENT VIRAL</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">With a mere handful of businesses, Pomfret, located in Vermont (US), is generally a quiet place. But in autumn, as “leaf-</li>
  <li>peepers” from everywhere descend on the region’s hills to witness its multicolored leaves, all that changes.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Ever since images of Sleepy Hollow Farm, a private property set on a rustic road, began going viral on social media a few</li>
  <li>years ago, locals say that things have gotten out of hand. A quick look on Instagram reveals thousands of images of the farm’s</li>
  <li>winding road lined by trees lit up in autumnal reds and pumpkin oranges. It’s no wonder then that this unlikely farm has become</li>
  <li>known as one of “the most photographed places in the state.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">Unfortunately, social media influencers regularly climb over gates covered with “No Trespassing” signs, get their “city cars”</li>
  <li>stuck on the narrow country road, and leave bodily waste by the roadside. “It’s a hotel and amusement park —bring all your friends,”</li>
  <li>laughed out an Instagrammer with 20,000 followers.</li>
  <li class="is-start">“It’s a beautiful spot. It’s too bad that it’s been ruined for everybody,” said Deborah Goodwin, a local councilwoman. “Tour</li>
  <li>buses are constantly unloading crowds of people out there. We can’t have this anymore.” In 2022, town officials finally voted to</li>
  <li>close the roads leading to the farm to non-residents during the autumn season, which annoyed the travelers who had come hoping</li>
  <li>to capture a perfect autumnal photo.</li>
  <li class="is-start">“We enjoy having tourists. It’s a big part of the Vermont economy, and we want people to enjoy its bucolic beauty and visit the</li>
  <li>shops,” Goodwin says. Her call is for responsible tourism that enjoys Pomfret’s beauty without ruining what makes it special —its</li>
  <li>peaceful charm.</li>
</ol>

<p class="en-task">CHOOSE AND WRITE THE CORRECT OPTION (A, B, C or D). (0.5 points each)</p>

<p class="en-question"><strong>9. According to the text, Pomfret has…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) always been a top tourist destination.</span>
  <span>(b) become an attraction due to media exposure.</span>
  <span>(c) been closed to tourists for economic reasons.</span>
  <span>(d) improved the tourist offer in autumn.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(b) become an attraction due to media exposure.</strong></p>
  <p>Las fotos de la granja se hicieron virales en las redes sociales y desde entonces la situación se ha desbordado (líneas 3-4): <em>“Ever since images of Sleepy Hollow Farm… began going viral on social media a few years ago, locals say that things have gotten out of hand.”</em></p>
  <p>Normalmente es un lugar tranquilo (<em>“generally a quiet place”</em>) (a); se cerraron las carreteras a los no residentes por las aglomeraciones, no por motivos económicos, y de hecho el turismo es importante para su economía (c); y el texto no habla de mejorar la oferta turística (d).</p>
</Solucion>

<p class="en-question"><strong>10. According to the text, “leaf-peepers” are…</strong></p>

<div class="en-options">
  <span>(a) visitors who love the colorful autumn countryside.</span>
  <span>(b) business owners who promote sustainable tourism.</span>
  <span>(c) tourists who are in favor of the recent ban.</span>
  <span>(d) councilors who welcome rural tourism.</span>
</div>

<Solucion>
  <p><strong>(a) visitors who love the colorful autumn countryside.</strong></p>
  <p>Son visitantes que llegan de todas partes para ver los colores de las hojas en otoño (líneas 1-2): <em>“‘leaf-peepers’ from everywhere descend on the region’s hills to witness its multicolored leaves”</em>.</p>
  <p>No son dueños de negocios (b) ni concejales (d), sino visitantes de fuera; y a los turistas la prohibición les molestó (<em>“which annoyed the travelers”</em>) (c).</p>
</Solucion>
