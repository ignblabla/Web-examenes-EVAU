<p class="en-title">El examen consta de 3 Bloques (A, B y C)</p>

En cada bloque (**Comprehension, Use of English** y **Writing**) se plantean varias preguntas, de las que se deberá responder al número que se indica en cada uno. En caso de responder más cuestiones de las requeridas, serán tenidas en cuenta las respondidas en primer lugar hasta alcanzar dicho número. Las preguntas han de ser respondidas en su totalidad: si la pregunta tiene dos secciones, hay que responder ambas.

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: THE HISTORY OF JEANS</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">The word <em>jeans</em> started out in the 1800s, in reference to a cotton cloth used for trousers. Blue jeans, now also called <em>denim</em>,</li>
  <li>were originally made from this fabric and manufactured in the French town of Nîmes. By the 20<sup>th</sup> century, <em>jeans</em> was the term for</li>
  <li>denim informal trousers.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The most recognizable, classic jeans were patented in 1873 by Jacob Davis, a tailor, and Levi Strauss, owner of a fabric house</li>
  <li>in San Francisco. Strauss and Davis initially made jeans in two types of fabric, brown cotton and blue denim, but the creation of</li>
  <li>the denim 501 style in 1890 helped the latter fabric take off.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Hollywood helped romanticize blue jeans in the 1920s and 1930s by putting the trousers on handsome cowboy types played</li>
  <li>by actors John Wayne and Gary Cooper. This glamorous new image appealed to consumers who sought casual leisurewear for</li>
  <li>the weekends and holidays. Publicity photos of famous actresses like Ginger Rogers and Carole Lombard wearing jeans helped</li>
  <li>convince women that the style was for them too. In the 1930s, <em>Vogue</em> gave their approval, calling jeans “Western chic”.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Yet, it wasn’t until the 1950s that jeans came to be associated with rebellious youth. Hippies wore jeans in the 1960s and early</li>
  <li>1970s as a way to show support for the working class, while feminists and women’s rights activists chose blue jeans as a way to</li>
  <li>demonstrate gender equality.</li>
  <li class="is-start">“I have often said that I wish I had invented blue jeans,” Yves Saint Laurent told <em>New York Magazine</em> in November 1983. “They</li>
  <li>have expression, modesty, sex appeal, simplicity… all that I hope for in my clothes.”</li>
</ol>
