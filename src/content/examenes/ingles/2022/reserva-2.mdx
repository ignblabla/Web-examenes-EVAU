<p class="en-title">El examen consta de 3 Bloques (A, B y C)</p>

En cada bloque (**Comprehension, Use of English** y **Writing**) se plantean varias preguntas, de las que se deberá responder al número que se indica en cada uno. En caso de responder más cuestiones de las requeridas, serán tenidas en cuenta las respondidas en primer lugar hasta alcanzar dicho número. Las preguntas han de ser respondidas en su totalidad: si la pregunta tiene dos secciones, hay que responder ambas.

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">People who come to Dublin for the first time may be surprised by the huge number of street performers or buskers, especially</li>
  <li>on the tourist-packed Grafton Street. They have become part of Dublin’s cultural life.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Musicians from all over the world compete to be heard on Grafton Street, playing everything from authentic Irish folk to modern</li>
  <li>punk. Playing on Grafton Street isn’t like any other busking experience. It’s one of the busiest streets in Europe, with over 9,000</li>
  <li>pedestrians passing through every hour —each one could be receptive or rude, generous or quite the opposite.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The increased popularity of busking in Dublin has also brought with it legislation; performers pay for the privilege to play on</li>
  <li>Grafton Street (a permit costs €30 a year) and follow strict regulations. Each busker can only play for one hour before moving on</li>
  <li>and can’t return within 100 metres of any of their previous locations until the following day.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The musicians queue, sometimes for hours, for one of the best-designated spots. They’re also in direct competition with other</li>
  <li>buskers along the street playing at the same time, which ensures that everyone who performs here puts their full heart and soul</li>
  <li>into it.</li>
  <li class="is-start">A unique interrelation between buskers and fans occurs only in the streets, with no security staff, no VIP seats, or entrance</li>
  <li>fee. Christmas Eve, for instance, is a really good time to visit Grafton Street, as megastars like Bono and Glen Hansard have been</li>
  <li>known to turn up and play for charity. If you like what you’re hearing, leave a tip —€2 or €3 is typical, but more is obviously</li>

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: A THOUSAND HUTS</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">Sustainability is a hot topic in the world of travel right now, but what does it actually mean? Everyone seems to have a different</li>
  <li>idea about what we should focus on. From electric cars and bicycles to slow food and zero-plastic hotels, there are countless</li>
  <li>factors that make a trip more sustainable. That’s where Scotland’s 1,000 Hut programme comes in. This initiative started in 2011,</li>
  <li>after a brainstorming session among Scottish environmental activists. Since then, they have been working with Reforesting</li>
  <li>Scotland to make cabins an alternative holiday destination, particularly for eco-conscious travellers who are fond of a minimalist</li>
  <li>holiday.</li>
  <li class="is-start">It could be a positive change in the way we holiday from now on, the initiative believes. Rather than trying to organise one</li>

  <li>need a break from the frenetic pace of urban life.</li>
  <li class="is-start">What the programme hopes is that “people will learn about and engage with nature, taking responsibility for the environment.”</li>
  <li>The experience includes travelling to the cabins in low-carbon, sustainable ways that could make visitors more appreciative of the</li>
  <li>world around them. Besides, it’s also a way of promoting local travel.</li>

  <li>more money flowing into those communities, and the more people are enjoying the countryside, the more financially equipped we’ll</li>
