<p class="en-title">El examen consta de 3 Bloques (A, B y C)</p>

En cada bloque (**Comprehension, Use of English** y **Writing**) se plantean varias preguntas, de las que se deberá responder al número que se indica en cada uno. En caso de responder más cuestiones de las requeridas, serán tenidas en cuenta las respondidas en primer lugar hasta alcanzar dicho número. Las preguntas han de ser respondidas en su totalidad: si la pregunta tiene dos secciones, hay que responder ambas.

<ol class="en-text">

<p class="en-heading en-gap">TEXT 2: GARUM IN THE 21<sup>st</sup> CENTURY</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">In modern laboratories, scientists are working to recreate <em>garum</em>, a sauce made from putrefying fish that was beloved by the</li>
  <li>ancient Greeks and Romans but hardly sounds appealing to 21st-century food lovers.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The Latin term <em>garum</em> could refer to both a sauce used in the cooking process —sometimes also called <em>liquamen</em>— and to a</li>
  <li>condiment, made with the blood and viscera of fish, that writers such as Petronius, Ausonius and Seneca knew as <em>garum sociorum</em></li>
  <li>(“garum of the allies”).</li>
  <li class="is-start">Archaeologists had been unsuccessful in extracting well-preserved <em>garum</em> at excavations in Tunisia and other Mediterranean</li>
  <li>locations until Italian researchers in 2009 discovered six sealed <em>dolia</em> —large clay amphoras— in a building that they named the</li>
  <li>Garum Shop at Pompeii. The eruption of Mount Vesuvius in AD 79 had buried the building under ash, thus perfectly preserving a</li>
  <li>small <em>liquamen</em> factory.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Food technicians from the universities of Cadiz and Seville analysed the contents of the <em>dolia</em> at Pompeii and, using that</li>
  <li>information and a <em>liquamen</em> recipe thought to have been written in the third century AD, produced what they claim is the first</li>
  <li>scientific recreation of the 2,000-year-old fish sauce. This recreation is called <em>Flor de Garum</em> and is being sold in amphora-shaped</li>
  <li>glass bottles throughout Spain. Chefs state that it supercharges dishes with a potent flavour.</li>
  <li class="is-start">It has been said that it is impossible to recreate definitive modern versions of these Roman ancient fish sauces, but at least</li>
  <li>this is the first time in modern history that a scientific reconstruction of <em>garum</em> has been successfully achieved.</li>
</ol>
