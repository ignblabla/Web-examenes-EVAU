<p class="en-heading">RENT A GRANDMA</p>

<ol class="en-text">
  <li class="is-start">According to Japan’s Ministry of Internal Affairs, around 9.1 million people aged 65 or older are still working. That’s</li>
  <li>approximately one in four seniors—many seeking extra income to supplement modest pensions. For older women, the challenge</li>
  <li>is especially demanding: many never had access to higher education or long-term employment and now struggle to find meaningful</li>
  <li>work later in life. To address this, a Tokyo-based company has created a unique initiative called <em>OK Obaachan</em>, which translates</li>
  <li>to ‘OK Granny.’ The project allows people to “rent” a grandmother for an hourly fee, offering both practical help and companionship</li>
  <li>while giving older women renewed purpose.</li>
  <li class="is-start">The OK Obaachan program is run by Client Partners, a firm dedicated to connecting women of all ages and backgrounds with</li>
  <li>job opportunities. Describing itself as a “women-only company,” it manages around 100 women aged 60 to 94 in the OK Obaachan</li>
  <li>division. No degree or professional experience is required—only the wisdom and empathy gained through a lifetime of experience.</li>
  <li>“Many people want to continue contributing to society as they get older,” says the company. “And the experience and abilities of</li>
  <li>older people are of great use to society.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">At a fixed rate of 3,300 yen per hour (about €19), clients can hire a grandma for a wide variety of tasks: cooking lessons,</li>
  <li>babysitting, household chores, or simply having someone to talk to. Others reach out for advice on relationships, family conflicts,</li>
  <li>or even heartbreak. The OK Obaachan website lists a range of stories: grandmas who helped mediate family disputes, taught new</li>
  <li>mothers how to care for babies, attended weddings in place of missing relatives, or cheered for children at school events.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Not everyone can join the program. The ideal OK granny, according to Client Partners, is kind, dependable, and hardworking,</li>
  <li>with an open mind. That last quality is key, as some clients seek help in situations such as single motherhood or divorce, which</li>
  <li>conservative Japanese society still views critically.</li>
  <li class="is-start">Beyond its novelty, OK Obaachan touches on a deeper social issue. In a society where isolation is rising and family ties are</li>
  <li>weakening, many older women feel left behind. Combined with sexism often masked as tradition and persistent gender</li>
  <li>expectations, that sense of invisibility can be profound. For these women, the project offers more than extra income—it restores a</li>
  <li>sense of <em>ikigai</em>, or “reason for being.”</li>
  <li class="is-start">As Japan ages, initiatives like OK Obaachan remind society that experience, kindness, and connection remain invaluable—no</li>
  <li>matter how old you are.</li>
</ol>
